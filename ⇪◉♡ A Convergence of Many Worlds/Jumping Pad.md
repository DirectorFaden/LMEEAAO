__JUMPING PAD__

It's 2023. The stubble on my face that grew during my sleepless night is giving me gender dysphoria. i’m fist deep in a family sized bag of Doritos, watching the Bob Ross channel on my TV as my bank account shrinks. My long-distance partner Gus is playing online video games, texting me between rounds with even less in their bank. Happy little trees painted 40 years ago are the only thing keeping me from a depressive spiral. Whatever love i had for Gus is gone but being alone in my transition scares me worse than reworking my resume for the 47th time, only to tell some interviewer my pronouns again. i’m on the brink of total collapse. 

i started dating Gus because we shared a love for escaping reality through video games and movies. We would get so excited exploring other worlds together! Over time i figured out that was all Gus had to offer. Money dwindled, we argued more, and the sex got worse. The relationship came to an end when i felt smothered and they threatened suicide if i left. i risked it and left for my best friend Melissa’s place in Los Angeles with a heart full of sorrow and eyes full of tears. Gus called and texted me the whole drive up and only stopped when Melissa called them and told them she would call the cops. i could finally breathe and let my anxious heart rest.

The next day, Melissa took me out to do something fun which for us means something macabre and supernatural. The Crooked Path, a nearby magic apothecary, checked both boxes. Staffed by people who regularly study and practice their crafts, the vibe there was mellow but meaningful. After wandering around a bit, feeling emotionally overwhelmed from the previous day i asked the staff for advice: “What can i do to help me express my creativity?” All my life my nervous system was constantly stressed, keeping me stuck in survival mode. i didn’t have the bandwidth to be creative. i slowly began relaxing when i started my transition but couldn’t find the right medium for me. After explaining this to an employee they gave me a candle and candle holder, oil, herbs, and instructions for use. They made sure i understood what i was asking for, instilling a sense in me that magic wasn’t something to take lightly. Skeptical, forewarned but willing to try, i bought the items. 

i mourned the loss of my relationship. i was in a fog for days, listless and distraught. In my stupor i decided to perform the candle magic ritual. i took the supplies out of their bag and lay them on a sheet of wax paper on my dingy bedroom floor, sitting in front of it. My hands were shaking as i put my whole heart and focus into the ritual. i let that nervous energy radiate through my body as i rolled the candle in the mixture of oil and herbs. i placed the candle gently in its holder and breathed steadily. i spread my legs open and let them border the wax paper, inviting a maternal, sapphic energy. Finally, i called on my artistic being, lit the candle, and watched it burn. The flame rose into its full power with flutters and flashes, herbs crackling on the adorned candle as they burned. After it was done i expected to feel significantly different. i didn’t feel anything! i got up, threw everything away except the candle holder, and slept.

Witchcraft didn’t pan out right away so i went back to distracting myself with whatever caught my attention. It was the night of the Oscars and hype for _Everything Everywhere All At Once_ was at a fever pitch. i was slightly interested before but with all the hype i needed to understand why people cared about it. Bored with nothing else to do, i turned on my TV and watched the movie.

From the first frame i found myself invested in the lives of the Wang family. Evelyn, the main character and matriarch, struggles her way through an inspiring journey. Initially her pursuit of a better life is externally focused; Evelyn wants to free her daughter of an evil entity that lives inside of her. By the end, Evelyn understands the only way she can help her daughter is to help herself grow and change. Her new way of life embraces absurdity and makes the Wang family better because of it. 

The parallels in _Everything Everywhere_ to my own life shocked me. i saw parts of myself in all the Wang family, i saw MY family in the Wangs. But what resonated deepest were my experiences with Gus’s family. Just like Evelyn’s daughter, Gus was also a second generation Chinese person who dealt with an overbearing, emotionally uninvested mother. And like the daughter’s girlfriend i am also half white, half Mexican. My familiarity with Chinese culture, family dynamics, and lifestyle was at an all-time high. As the movie ended i wept uncontrollably for what felt like hours, allowing all the tension from my relationship to pour out of me in a flood of emotional release. 

The next morning i went out and bought a physical copy of the movie. i picked it apart to find every hidden detail. There was a driving force in me i had never felt before. With each watch i was discovering a secret world that was hiding in plain sight.

On my seventh day with the movie, i decided i would see it in theatres. i bought a ticket for the first screening and wrote on my Instagram story that i was hoping for some peace and relaxation after such a rough period of time. A new kind of peace was coming for me, but in an unexpected way.

i settled in my seat while the previews played, and around 30 people trickled in. My excitement and joy grew as the movie played. People were laughing and gasping along with me. About 45 minutes in, everything changed.

On the screen was a scene where Evelyn and her other universe husband are talking, “You’ve been feeling it too, haven’t you? Our institutions are crumbling. Nobody trusts their neighbor anymore. And you stay up at night thinking to yourself…” “How do we get back?” Evelyn responds. 

A man in the audience a few rows behind me stood up and walked down the seating stairs. He was followed closely by a woman who was trying to pull him by the arm towards the entrance ramp to talk to him in private. With a swift tug he pulled away from her and stood dead center in the middle aisle between the lower and upper seats of the theatre.

He began to speak, loudly enough for everyone to hear him: “I am a schizophrenic. I know I'm in this movie right now.” Then, speaking nonchalantly to the woman with him said, “Oh god, I'm going to murder you, aren’t I?” The entire theatre seemed to vibrate with shock. The movie had put him into a mental health crisis. The woman dragged the man more forcibly toward the entrance ramp. i did my best to listen as she tried to calm him down. 

This woman’s footsteps rang through the theatre as she sprinted down the entrance ramp, the man’s’ footsteps pounding behind her. She screamed before the door at the end of the ramp slammed against the wall. Five of us jumped out of our seats and ran after them both. The emergency fire extinguisher mounted near the exit was missing. i heard the woman scream again.

Bursting out of the theatre door, i saw the woman lying on her back on the floor, the man straddled at her waist, the fire extinguisher in his hands as he used it as a blunt weapon against this woman’s head. 

Without thinking i sprinted the 20 feet towards them both and tackled the man off of the woman. As we recovered from the tackle the man reached for the extinguisher again, presumably to use against me. i saw this, pulled the extinguisher away from him, and rolled it out of reach for us both. i then forced him onto his stomach, pressed my hands against his back, and kept him in place until help could arrive. During the fray the woman took refuge against a set of locked doors at the front entrance.

The man was detained while the woman was being treated by emergency medical services. With my adrenaline still pumping, i tried to get the cops' attention so i could give my statement. If i were any other person they would have approached me. Instead, as a visible transsexual, the cops kept walking away in displays of feigned busyness so they wouldn’t have to speak with me. On the verge of tears, i asked an employee to help me. 

The two of us walked into the lobby. The employee was a thin, caucasian male who caught a cops’ attention immediately. With the theatre management in the lobby watching the scene and the employee pointing right at me, the cop begrudgingly interacted with me. 

The schizophrenic man had his arms behind him cuffed tightly with a chain lead attached. The detaining cop who held the lead stood next to him and let the suspect lunge towards me multiple times while I gave my statement. My blood ran cold watching this cop smile while treating this man like a rabid dog. The combination of rage and fear i felt destabilized me even more, and entrenched my disrespect for cops.

After giving my statement i left. i got home and started crying and dissociating. i could feel something happening to my brain. The pain and remorse of the theatre incident collided with the stress of my recent breakup. My mental health deteriorated rapidly while my mind started expanding. i was “seeing” far beyond this reality to realities that existed elsewhere. i began noticing synchronicities. Discerning between realities was nearly impossible. 

That night, my head and heart inundated with physical and existential pain, i slept for thirteen hours in a dark night of the soul. Every hurt i’ve ever had was re-experienced in my dreams. i was spiraling and couldn’t see a way out until finally a spark sat in the middle of my inner vision. i concentrated on it, and when it got too bright to handle i opened my eyes. 

i knew for a fact i was an entirely different person. i could feel that spark from my dream become a tiny ember that sat near my heart, as though an altar had been built for it there. i sensed this ember could be fueled through my lifestyle and choices, and eventually fanned into flames of creativity.

In the moments after a heroic act i was seen as subhuman because i’m transsexual. My journey since isn’t defined by a lack of recognition for what i’ve done. It’s defined by how far society has to go before it embraces a diversity of human life. If saving someone else’s life isn’t enough, what WILL it take to recognize the shared humanity of trans people?

If it weren’t for transitioning, dating Gus, practicing magic, or watching _Everything Everywhere All At Once_, i wouldn’t have built my own understanding of reality. i experience life as a multiversal being, someone whose body and mind are a vessel for every version of me i’ve ever dreamed of and many i haven’t. i am a conduit for my creativity, staying out of the way of the work as it flows through me. But most importantly, i’ve discovered my authenticity and my humanity. Everyone deserves to find theirs in whatever ways they want. i’m doing my part to help by building futures that get us back to connection and love, one sentence at a time.
