i’ve often seen transitioning one’s sex:gender equated with a “second puberty.” In many ways that’s true. The body can change shape, the mind can go through phases reminiscent of child-like and teenage years. Even bodily scents change. Over time, the comfort one can gain from altering their physical being can change their personality significantly. Self confidence rises. New social skills are learned. Relationships to oneself and those closest can contract or expand, depending.

To me, this is only half of the story. The other half is a total change of mindset. A new mental:emotional outlook develops as the body develops in kind. It feels like a slow rebirth. Most people will never understand just how dramatic a change transition can be.

When your body and mind don’t line up the way either one expects, you’re caught in a trap of nature’s faulty design. To borrow a concept from The Matrix, it’s a problem of Residual Self Image. In the case of transition, this is a mismatch between your internal mental projection of your physical self and your physical reality. You’ll follow the expectations others set for you based solely on your physical reality so you have a sense of comfort, meaning, and purpose. You’ll find reasons to fade into the background. Shrinking yourself is the norm. 

But when you’re able to take your life into your own hands, you’ll find it’s expansive. The ways trans people experiment with themselves brings forward a greater understanding of its own. Your new attitude can draw the right people to you. You’ll find ease in places you previously found resistance. Goals can be set and reached. Satisfying lusts and deep loves are within your grasp. 

This kind of incredible change has a scientific study dedicated to it: neuroplasticity. This is the study of how the brain is capable of change, of learning new things, of adapting to thrive. At specific points in our growth from infancy to adulthood, aspects of neuroplasticity reach their peaks. My physically mature transsexual brain before transition was a mire of despair. Its ability to change was as “set” as any other adult. As neuroplasticity teaches us, the brain isn’t “set” in its ways at all! Brains are able to change and adapt at any time. After starting transition (it’s my opinion transition never truly ends), the brain must find a renewed plasticity to cope with new connections and changes.

As for why i identify as transsexual instead of transgender: During my initial stages of transition i was taking slow steps to discover who i truly was. At the time that included adapting myself into our cultural expectations of the gender binary: women and men. Since i was transitioning from a man to a woman i identified as a transgender woman. Over time i discovered how uncomfortable i was performing gender in the ways society expects women to. i was uncomfortable wearing make-up. i didn’t enjoy vocal training myself to sound more feminine as it felt more performative than genuine. Adapting how i walk to be more feminine was like constantly walking on a tightrope. And that tightrope feeling eventually permeated every aspect of my changing gender performance. [i currently do not identify with any gender expression and reject the very idea of gender and a binary. As a result, i am not non-binary and i do not identify with any gender pronouns including they/them.  My preference is to be called Kelly instead of using a pronoun. For example, “Kelly rode Kelly’s bike to the beach.” i am Kelly Lavigne, human being.]

How else can i explain the way i acted as each year of transition passed into the next? During these years, i felt everything with tremendous depth. Too often i was unable to regulate my emotions. Before HRT (hormone replacement therapy) i had no ability to access my emotions. My social affect was flat. i was unable to cry. i never knew what to say or when to say it. How much of this was because of untreated complex trauma? How much was from a body and brain that didn’t recognize each other? Who’s to say.

Regardless, over the course of my transition i felt more comfort than i had ever known. If the body is capable of changes with the introduction of foreign hormones, it follows that the brain would change in kind. Learning an entirely new way to live can enable the brain to change dramatically. 

Think about a routine you’ve added to your life that enhanced it. Maybe you’ve added a morning walk to your routine before work or started writing a journal page each night before bed. That’s a new way of life on its own. Now imagine adopting the opposite gender scripts of every interaction you’ve ever known. You’d never get them right to start but as you learned, you’d change. Change is internal, transmitted from brain to body and back again. If you keep it consistent, that’s practiced plasticity. Deep into my thirties and living full-time as an out transsexual, there was a glimmer of hope i might become the person i had always dreamed of.
