**⇪ Circuit I : Oral Bio-Survival**

---

**Physical Location** - Hindbrain, Autonomic Nervous System 

**Functions** - Self-Preservation

**Relation to Time** - n/a 

The circuits build on top of one another with time and understanding. As such, Circuit I is the foundation. No person who has ever lived has been without access to this most basic form of consciousness:understanding. It’s mainly concerned with survival:self preservation. We are made to either move towards comfort or move away from danger at our core. 

The problem with such a basic on:off system is its reliance on subconscious twitch reactions that put clarity of deeper understanding and judgment on the proverbial back burner. The nervous system forces the body to react as quickly as possible to either protect the body or meet its needs without conscious thought.

For Circuit I, RAW places the physical location at “the brainstem and in the autonomic (involuntary) nervous system where it is interconnected with the endocrine and other life support systems.” The brainstem or hindbrain makes the connection between the brain and the rest of the body through its direct connection to the physical nervous system as a whole at the medulla.

Also in the hindbrain is the cerebellum. Though it rests in the far back of the physical brain, it connects to the frontal cortex which allows the brain to execute quick motor function movements based on the information the frontal cortex is taking in. The cerebellum learns how to move the body as it experiences more demand, creating programs that communicate between the two sections, then executes the programs as needed. The more the need for movement is present, the more the cerebellum changes and refines the movement programs. The pons, also located in this area, acts like a bridge between the mid- and hindbrain. It aids with functions that control both arousal and sleep. 

Most importantly is that Circuit I functions mainly based on the Autonomic Nervous System (ANS). The main function of the ANS is to monitor:maintain the kinds of body functions we never have to think about. Things like blood pressure and body temperature are under its control. When these and other functions aren’t within acceptable levels, it's the ANS’ job to try to regulate them and bring them to a known comfortable baseline.

That the ANS is part of Circuit I is no coincidence. The ANS is an extremely valuable system that was developed to maintain the brain:body long before cognition was within human capability. It’s the evolutionary call to full-body calm or excitement based on what’s happening to or around someone. The ability of the ANS is homeostasis, the point at which an equilibrium between function, energy use, and alertness is found within the body and all of its parts. The ANS moves the signals between the organs and the demands of the body to a meeting point. From here it activates either the sympathetic or parasympathetic nervous system and increases or decreases the function of organs and muscles based on the body’s needs, respectively. That’s why part of the name of this Circuit is Bio-Survival. Our biological needs drive the rest of our physical systems. It gives us the ability to create positive conditions for ourselves on the outside of our bodies while it handles the inner needs without ourconscious input.

The “oral” part of Circuit I’s name is as base as it sounds. As living creatures, we have a need for nourishment that must be answered in order for our bodies to survive. Eating sustains the body, giving it energy to use all its internal systems (including the brain and ANS) to reach and maintain homeostasis. When we’re infants, our survival is often dependent on our mother. We may breastfeed which triggers this oral:bio-survival connection directly. 

It’s hardwired in each of us to seek the round and pleasing form of the breast, RAW argues. Art from time immemorial depicts breasts if not only to show their ability to nourish, but sometimes simply for aesthetic pleasure. “As for music – where did we first hear it, who sang or hummed it to us, and against what part of her body were we held?” Further he cites Freud and his assertion that opiates can trigger neuro-transmitters that bring us mentally back to the comfort and security of the breast.

RAW explains: “The bio-survival circuit is DNA-programmed to seek a comfort-safety zone around a mothering organism. If a mother isn’t present, the closest substitute in the environment will be imprinted.” “...the imprint is made by chance...some [people] imprint bravery, inquisitiveness, and the exploratory drive; others imprint timidity, infophobia (fear of the unpredictable) and withdrawl…, ” And so it was with my youth the imprint of the former in the digital world and the latter in the physical world.

Video games, my digital opiate, gave me the comfort and safety i rarely knew from either of my parental figures. The roundness of the screen of my television made its form even more appealing, as it subconsciously evoked the image of a breast. My brain, already under the influence of drugs made to numb it to stimulus for its own safety, needed more attention and care than was available to me. The realities of the systems we survive under that keep parents constantly busy as well as the conflicts that arose between my parental figures personally only set my ANS up to understand that all the stimulus around it in the physical world was something to fear:withdraw from. There was never an opportunity to bring my body into homeostasis.

In my opinion, this was all the more reason i found a sense of calm when i was playing video games. As i explained earlier, the stimulus from video games does cause stress but it’s a kind of reliable stress that can be predicted. The predictability of stress is something that can help the ANS learn what the acceptable baseline of functions connected to the system are. By playing video games i was not only keeping my brain from making too many connections and overloading, but was further teaching it what homeostasis looked like for me when exposed to predictable stress.

i became the product of the digital age in the truest sense: only through digital stimulation did my brain:body learn physical regulation since the physical environment outside itself was too unpredictable:dangerous to ever get a reading that could set the baseline for homeostasis needs. It’s no surprise this happened to me and in fact may have saved me from even more debilitating mental traits. The pediatrician who suggested the video game treatment would have had no idea what my home life was like. Regardless, video games gave me a chance to experience a kind of safety (albeit misguided) that i otherwise never would have known.

In short, our instinctual selves at the most foundational:basic level is a pure need for self preservation. Our need to protect ourselves isn’t as obvious as it might seem. Sure, none of us want to die. But have you ever thought about WHY you don’t want to die?

No, I don’t mean this in a “you’re being counseled in a crisis and need a distraction” kind of way. I mean, what drives humans:humanity to survive? At THIS foundational level, survival is about genetic security. Those who are unable:unwilling to move beyond Circuits I-IV are most often driven to procreate, historically speaking. 

When humanity was made to survive in more trying times, having more children to care for was initially a heavy burden, but the hope (if early humanity understood such a thing) was that in time all of those offspring would provide for the unable:elderly of the tribe. This lack of environmental equilibrium never went away, and neither did the expectations that came with it.
	
Here in the reality of the “first”:“civilized” world, we are made to reside mainly in the fear state:survival mode of Circuit I. Each of us, instead of relying on the collective power of a local tribe composed of many families working together to provide each other with resources:safety, has been made into “individuals” who act independently from one another. In our modern lives we have become even further separated from each other based on prejudices made by other people for their personal gain.

Instead of tribal reliance, we are told to build “self reliance.” Instead of finding equilibrium, we are told to consume with abandon. And in the midst of this consumption we must teach ourselves to often withdraw from our “need” for it (a laughable concept at best, as our social system has created this artificial concept of a “need” to gather:collect:consume trivial things) so that we can remain “self reliant.” In this state of chasing the constantly moving goal post that means “surviving,” is it any wonder so many of us live in anxiety:panic on a daily basis?

RAW explains that bio-survival fears are something that can be overcome in a “technology of abundance.” Sadly that would only be true if that technology, especially what we have now, was utilized with the intention of increasing the well-being of one another. Instead, technology is another way to distract us from the physical worlds in front of us. Our technologies stress us and add to the gap of the haves and have-nots.

Physical interaction with our selves, other people, and our environments have instead been replaced with electronics and rectangular screens of varying sizes, occasional meaningless physicality with others, and the gripping of vehicle steeringwheels:computer mice. We have replaced the real with the artificial; the simulacra for the simulation. All of us are guilty of living on the map and not in the territory. It has become easier to distract ourselves with a digital opiate than to work towards collective betterment:awakening.

RAW also asserts that our modern understanding of survival has been bastardized by money, or as he likes to call it, tickets. “Bio-survival depends on getting the tickets.” He connects the idea further to our loss of tribal support, saying that if any of us were cut off from our ability to collect money:tickets, it would be the same as being thrown out of a tribe meant for support. This is the only way to ensure people will play the larger game of the economy: survival is based on an individual’s ability to collect tickets in order to survive. 

Giving people the ability to survive with free shelter and free food destroys the entire point of the economy. The way our current system is set up, without forcing people to survive on their own by collecting tickets, by sacrificing their bodies, minds, and time, what would the incentive for working be? Would anyone be willing to do the menial labor it takes to maintain our current system? How does an economy driven by fear force people to collect tickets if they don’t need to collect tickets in order to survive?

It doesn’t.

Finally, i mentioned earlier that Circuit I relies on near-instant reactions in order for the body to survive threats or gain resources. One of the strongest threads throughout the work of PR is each Circuit’s relation to time. Does each Circuit understand time? Do the Circuits have different ways of utilizing such a complicated construct? They do! In fact, Circuit I has the distinction of having no concept of time whatsoever.

Think about what it means to survive in a primitive sense. Your ability to react quickly to threats:opportunities could determine whether you live or die. If, in this state, you had any concept of time at all you might miss your chance to react appropriately and die anyway. So it is with Circuit I: the Circuit is built to act unconsciously, with no concept of time, in order to survive. In a state of panic or fear, the last thing any animal has time to do is think. Time isn’t necessary.

Until all the bio-survival needs of a person are met, no other Circuit can reasonably be considered active. Circuit I is the ultimate authority on living in the moment, because it doesn’t know what a moment is. “It is the sense of being here-now, in this vulnerable body, subject to the raw energies and forces of the physical universe.” The importance of homeostasis:stability is thus solidified. If you can’t make yourself comfortable, you can’t do anything else until you are.
