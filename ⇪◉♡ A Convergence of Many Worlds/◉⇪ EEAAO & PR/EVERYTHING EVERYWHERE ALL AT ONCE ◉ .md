Even if it’s never explicitly stated or shown, i found it easy to read Evelyn as a Gifted Child. On greating Evelyn into the world and being told her assigned sex, Gong-Gong's disappointment is clear to read on his face. The little we're shown of Evelyn growing up includes several instances of her father being over-bearing and controlling. Add in Evelyn's need to micromanage her life, the lives of her family members, and prove her value and worth to Gong-Gong as an upstanding mother and wife and we can see how much Evelyn has been molded by the expectations of her father. When someone has an expectation of greatness put onto them by their parents:guardians, they often shrink their own needs. Whether denying their physical needs through putting on a tough facade when they’re hurt or covering up emotional needs by never showing doubt, crying, or ever asking for help, a Gifted Child is a surrogate parent for themselves. Their humanity is expected to be simultaneously superhuman and non-existent, self-managed and invisible. Above all else, they are no longer allowed to be children in any way except for their physical size. They either have adult responsibilities placed onto them or may recognize the need for someone responsible to take charge of a situation and fill in the gap themselves. i strongly feel that Evelyn choosing to marry Waymond is her taking responsibility for his life before she made time to build her own. Cultural expectations of being a good wife and future mother likely played a part as well. Because she decided to marry Waymond and move to "America", her family disowned her. She wanted a better life and paid the price: Gong-Gong saw her choice as abandonment, so her abandoned her in return.

That feeling of abandonment works both ways. Though it isn’t shown with Evelyn (since EEAAO skips her teenage years), when a girl is born into some traditional Chinese households the expectation is to take on the feminine roles of the house. Cleaning, cooking, clothes mending, and any other “women’s work” is meant to be shared among the mother and any daughters of the home. In all likelihood Evelyn participated in these expectations either begrudgingly or not at all. The only evidence i have for this implication is how “Americanized” both Evelyn and Waymond look when Waymond is proposing marriage on the balcony in China. Chances are both of them had long abandoned traditional Chinese expectations by then. Gong-Gong disowned Evelyn for more than just marrying Waymond and leaving China, i’m sure. By that point, Evelyn likely hadn’t been keeping house with her aging mother for some time.

In my early stages of life and in Evelyn’s early stages in the film, we were both caught in strict, almost robotic cycles. We adopted:built these cycles as a means of self protection. We wanted to show that we had control of ourselves despite not knowing what self-control was. Every choice we made was from a place of fear, lack, doubt, and uncertainty. We never knew safety, so we did what little we could to feel safe. We learned that the only people we could trust and rely on were our selves.

How well Evelyn and i took care of ourselves was in direct response to our individual inabilities to work through the stresses of our lives. And since most of our lives were focused on how we interacted with people in our families, we rarely found space to process our feelings. Not that either of us knew how to process them anyway.

Luckily for Evelyn, she had a chance to grow into her life with guidance from both Alpha Waymond and Jobu Tupaki. Me? i wasn’t as lucky. My push into heightened consciousness wasn’t as guided. i was left to fend for my own well being, a choice that in the long term has proven to be the right move. At the time i was worried no one around me would understand what i was going through. i gave myself space from the people i was closest to so that i could protect both them and my selves from harm.

Conversely, Evelyn’s pain is met with kindness, challenge, understanding, and an interest in inclusion from her established family unit (...kind of). Any guidance i received from people in my life was rare. Much of my understanding came from within:beyond me later in life, led specifically by a chain of media i consumed that made me aware of the work of Robert Anton Wilson.
