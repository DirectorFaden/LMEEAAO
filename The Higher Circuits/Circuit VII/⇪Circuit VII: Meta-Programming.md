Everything i’ve learned about the Eight Circuits model of consciousness to this point has been an incredible boon to my life and the way i move in it. Every Circuit has a function that’s clear and meaningful. Each one serves several functions that make life easier to understand. Finally, after decades of struggle, i felt like i had found a way to live that could help me find stability and peace.

That’s why it was so hard realizing that all of it was just in my mind.

When it comes to the way each of us perceives and understands reality, each of us has learned different ways of coping with problems that arise. We have our struggles and vices, things we embrace and those we ignore. Whether we learned these things in life as an active participant or the lessons were thrust upon us, we learned to deal with (or not deal with) the issues we faced. When it comes to Western society, every single thing we’ve ever learned has been in service to upholding the system in which we live. From the moment of birth until you discover otherwise, each lesson has been about being the person the system wants you to be. How much of what you consider to be “you” is actually YOU? To put it more clearly, do you believe everything you’ve been taught by your caregivers, teachers, colleagues, and friends? What about what you were taught by your bullies and enemies? What has sunken in to the folds of your brain, shaped your mind, and made you the person you are today?

What if i told you nothing you define yourself as has to be who you are forever?

i grew up hearing people tell me that i can “be whatever i want to be”, that “the only thing stopping you is you”, that “the world is your oyster.” The people who told me these things were never the people who showed me any of that was actually possible. Is it any wonder why i started looking elsewhere for people to model myself after? The people closest to me worked dead-end jobs to survive. They never had space for other pursuits, or at least never made space for them. Their lives were dedicated to survival. They acted like they didn’t have a choice. The contradiction was obvious to me from a young age. Instead of it motivating me to do better than those who tried to placate me with these adages, i understood them as more instructions that fell under “do as i say, not as i do.” i couldn’t reconcile with the hypocrisy. Besides, i’ve always had issues with authority anyway. Actually taking any of that advice to heart was an uphill battle from the start.

But then my brain underwent a drastic change and my mind followed suit. It turns out that life really is just a game. No one knows what they’re doing. No one. If the opposite is something you’ve been told before by someone who claims to “have it all figured out,” let me help you: they’re full of shit (CII, anyone?). They don’t know what they’re doing any more than you do. What they might have is a direction they’re moving in. Don’t be fooled: their “forward” isn’t necessarily the only way forward (in fact, i can guarantee it’s not). More to the point, their “forward” doesn’t have to be your forward! 

RAW made this point slightly more eloquently when he quoted Nietzsche in PR: “We are all greater artists than we realize.” Still, my point stands. Each of us has the ability to modify our perceptions of reality as we see fit. The history of human civilization relies on each person willingly being part of the society each civilization is built upon. By being part of a society, we each agree to an implicit agreement to participate in the society and its systems as they already exist. This is a social contract. Often social contracts come with a cost of some kind for those that participate. One must work towards a certain end goal, sacrifice privacy for security, or otherwise give some governing body part of what you build:create:grow. 

Each person who participates in a society helps the ideas behind that society grow stronger. The agreement each person makes by engaging with that society gives the systems that govern that society a modicum of legitimacy that, over time, adds up. Eventually, and with enough willing participants, a society will gain a reputation greater than those who participate in it. The people born into the society after it’s established will more willingly believe in its legitimacy. This leads to a point where, after a few generations of this automatic participation in said society, the eldest generation will adopt the idea that “this is the way its always been” and defend that societies right to continue on as it is so that those eldest people won’t have to deal with the cognitive dissonance that comes with radical changes to their expectations around life and lifestyles.

This unfortunate line of thinking tends to embolden those least prone to question their stations in life. Those that don’t question, defend. Those that are greatly affected by the negative aspects of a society will embrace that society’s ways more readily than most, as long as their basic life needs are met:they can comfortably live in the Lower Circuit they prefer most. Anything that threatens the ability of these defenders to continue living the way they prefer will be a target for their territorial challenges. The systems that built the society that bred the people that defend it rely on those same people to keep those systems functioning. As long as the systems of a society keep its defenders satiated enough, the strength of the legitimacy of said society will remain strong.

At its core, a social contract is an agreed upon hallucination. Each person who buys into the society enhances the strength of the hallucination. As the hallucination grows, it becomes its own reality. And thus, that reality insulates those within it and tries to keep those people from seeing other ways of living. Societies are reality tunnels writ large. Just as each of us have built our own reality-tunnels within our brains:minds, every time a group of people assembles under one goal:understanding:flag a reality-tunnel then encompasses everyone under that one thing. Reality goes from being holistic to being gestalt. A society is something that is greater than the sum of its parts. It is the culmination of many different people:ideas gathering together to build a way of living that holds those ideas aloft.

On paper this sounds like a promising life. Everyone who wants to participate in this way of living has a chance to build a meaningful assemblage of humanity together. There’s a problem with this, though: every person is so different that true social cohesion (the idea that every person will think equally of the rules that govern a society) is completely impossible. Even if there were some society where every single person “followed the rules”, eventually there would be a few detractors that call for change or otherwise act towards creating it. RAW puts it this way: “...it is neurologically obvious that no two brains have the same genetically-programmed hard wiring, the same imprints, the same conditioning, the same learning experiences. We are all living in separate realities.” 

Though the illusion of a society is strong enough to convince the vast majority of people living in it that said society is legitimate simply because it exists, there will always be people who don’t want things to stay the same. The only reason these people continue to participate in this existing society is because they don’t have many other choices. If one chooses not to participate, life becomes hostile immediately. Those that uphold the law will try to find and detain you. Those outside the law will try to exploit or recruit you into their reality-tunnel. If you wander off on your own, you’ll have to rely on yourself, your cunning, and your ability to survive against the threats nature throws your way. The truth of the matter is that people need people. Societies and social contracts exist as a way to not only legitimize the ideas:ideals of systems but also to keep people continually feeding those systems and each other. Civilized humans can only practice civility in a civilization. Are you starting to feel the looping logic yet?

What those people who are rebelling against a society tend to have in common is their ability to “see” beyond the restrictions of the society in the first place. But in order to get to that place where they can “see beyond”, there must be a certain level of self-awareness. As i’ve mentioned, people become self-aware in only a few ways: either a person can learn and guide themselves into awareness through life experience and a deep personal understanding through introspection, or they can be forced into that place by a spiritual Awakening not unlike my own. RAW mentions that most people are forced into Awakening. i’m inclined to agree. Anyone:everyone is capable of getting to greater self-awareness. Sadly, most people won’t have the opportunity to think themselves into this kind of cognitive freedom due to the circumstances of their hardships and challenges in life. That’s the most likely reason for “awareness by force” in the first place: compounded life challenges and traumas that in time break people and make them new in one way or another, either with self-guidance or surrender. Despite the decades of pain it took for me to be reborn in this way, i’m grateful for having the opportunity at all. My life before wasn’t one worth living. It’s nothing short of miraculous that the brain is capable of such drastic change, and that i was lucky enough to benefit from it while still relatively young.

It has been my experience that my overarching goal with engaging in spirituality has been to seek a sense of belonging. All of us are social creatures who are made to engage with one another. Historically, we belonged to small groups:tribes where each of us had purpose and contributed to the overall good of that group. In the modern day, that human need to belong can guide us towards situations where we feel connected to others. In so doing, and by engaging with a found group, we open ourselves to being programmed by others.

For those who have struggled all their lives to find belonging and purpose, opening oneself up to engage with the ideas of others may not necessarily be a bad thing. While everyone is capable of thinking for themselves, some people may not want to think for themselves for many different reasons. As diverse a landscape as the human mind can be, for some that landscape may be more desolate minefield than verdant plain (or plane, as the case may be). When we understand that the mind, as its own plane of existence, can change with new information and under differing circumstances we understand how we are capable of being programmed and programming ourselves. Remember back to the opening chapters of this book: using computer language to describe our abilities is a false equivalency but a useful one. Your mind is not a computer, but we can think of it as acting as if it were a computer. We can change our minds, we can meta-program.

When a person, of their own free will, engages with a group they’re trying to be a part of, that person makes an effort to understand the way that group thinks. To willingly become part of the group is to engage in its emotional territory (think: Circuit II) New terminology and:or rules are often present that group members repeat and abide by (semantic challenges on Circuit III as well as mantra in Circuit V). Engaging with the new group’s rules, ideals, morality, and ethics engages all the major functions of Circuit IV. Rules around sexuality and sexual engagement may be present as well, thus solidifying the CIV functions as part of the group’s ideology. Most importantly however is for one to have a sense of safety and acceptance into any new group they choose to be part of. Circuit I is the baseline of all human behavior for a reason: we all wish for a sense of safety to be with us at all times. To be part of a group that has welcomed you with open arms and brought you into their fold is to give the CNS a chance to acclimate to new surroundings with a sense of ease. If those CI needs are met, it’s practically a guarantee that the solo answer seeker will become part of the group. In this way, becoming reprogrammed is just a matter of allowing whatever the rules of the group are to overtake any pre-existing ideas you may have about how you view the world.

If someone is determined to reprogram themselves though is entirely different. To successfully meta-program oneself takes a considerable amount of self awareness. How much do you know about you? Can you describe how you might react to different situations in detail? How do you engage with your emotions? With your body? Do you have people in your life that you trust or love? Are you more likely to face your fears or avoid them? Do you have an idea of how you present yourself to the world? How well do you engage with your sense of presence in general? What are some things that make you happy, sad, angry, afraid? How many of these questions can you answer? These inquiries and many others can help you establish just how aware of yourself you are. Being more or less aware isn’t necessarily good or bad (i do my best to avoid reducing things to that), it’s more a matter of gauging one’s sense of wholeness within themselves.

meditation is neurosomatic. It’s a means of allowing all parts of the self to exist simultaneously. The practice of meditation is to bring awareness to the whole of oneself without trying too hard to steer the mind in one direction or another. As one sits in meditation, allowing all feelings, thoughts, and sensations internal and external to coexist without trying to force change on any part is the practice. As one allows the practice to envelop them, the idea is to find rest and solace in a state of peaceful awareness. The closer one gets to a clear mind that’s focused on simply being, the more space one opens in their mind to allow for the rest to feel good, to enhance the sense of self.

Where it gets messy is that though the rulesets for the Circuits are static, the Circuits themselves are malleable. Can you remember all the way back to Circuit II, the four imprints? Hostile Strength, Friendly Strength, Hostile Weakness, and Friendly Weakness are examples of versions of Circuit II. Another way of talking about these versions is the term models. A model is related to theory of mind:the ability of one person to understand that other people experience consciousness differently than they do. To build a model using theory of mind is to build a specific way of experiencing consciousness and thus reality for an individual. RAW defines this as “neurological relativism”, equating it to other scientific definitions of relativism. 

Reality-tunnels, as i’ve explained them so far, are either an individuals or a group's perception of reality as ascribed by the rulesets they allow to govern that reality. People who are anarchists, Christians, fans of basketball, anyone who allows their reality to be shaped by a confluence of cultures and the ways they were raised or they have changed themselves are people who have, and live, in their own reality-tunnels. When a person jumps into Higher Circuitry, they’re jumping out of the restraints of their reality-tunnel and into a way of being that reaches beyond neurological relativism. What was once inside or outside of the self is no longer seen to be that way. “...the unity of the field of perception is then recognized. We become Metaprogrammers.”

It’s the brain and mind functioning together to create a model of a previous reality that one can use to inform themselves or others of the past they experienced. 

The words we use to describe our realities matter just as much as the models we inherit or create for ourselves. Those approximations we make about our reality demand an understanding of some kind. Language is often the most accessible solution here. But semantic choices (recall Circuit III) carry weight. The words we choose convey certain ideas we have about ourselves and our world. Semantic maps and interpretive maps work in tandem. Though one may not necessarily be born from the other, they do inform one another.
