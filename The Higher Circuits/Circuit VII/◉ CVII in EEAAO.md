When Evelyn learns to practice Kindness Kung-Fu, she’s using meta-programming on herself to change the way she interacts with others. The Alpha Jumpers are coming at Evelyn with all their might. Determined to destroy what they see as a threat to every universe, they’re carrying guns, swords, grenades, whips, and weapons of all kinds. Evelyn’s sudden understanding of what it means to change inside of oneself allows her to bring that understanding outward to those that would oppose her.

As the Alpha Jumpers approach her, Evelyn studies each one and in some cases, remembers interactions from her various pasts that give her guidance and insight into how best to help each one. In the case of two Jumpers, she decides to find the ‘verse where they fall in love and get married. The older man from the laundromat is enamored of Evelyn because of the perfume she wears (it was his wife’s, he’s a widower). She pulls the grenade he’s carrying towards her, jumps to a ‘verse where it’s a bottle of that perfume instead, and sprays the grenade directly in his face. He’s taken down by love as well. Evelyn even tells Alpha Gong-Gong about Joy being gay. He’s heartbroken by the news but no longer tries to attack after he learns the truth.

	For each of the Alpha Jumpers (yes, even the one where Evelyn practices chiropractic medicine), the defining factor is that love is the ultimate meta-programming tool. It’s only through love that any of us can change ourselves. It is the underlying factor that defines life. For all of its unpredictability, confusion, and chaos, love is a guiding force shared by every human (and arguably, all living things). 

  Evelyn chose to accept Jobu’s programming in order to get close to her, to try to understand what it would take to love her. The closer Evelyn got to Jobu, the closer she got to Joy. Eventually, from Temple to Rock, Jobu began to give Evelyn more information and connection. Jobu slowly opened up to Evelyn more and more, changing Evelyn as they went. Love is change. It’s never a static factor of anyone’s life. To love is to change just as being loved is to allow for change. Recalling that “no man is an island” gives us further insight into what it means to allow all things in our experience to affect us and change us. Openness is vulnerability in its purest form. 

	Unfortunately for Evelyn, Jobu isn’t entirely convinced that she needs to open up all the way. With every Alpha Jumper defeated, Jobu is left vulnerable to Evelyn’s oncoming attack. As Evelyn approaches, she and Jobu tussle until Jobu holds Evelyn against the swirling torrent of the Bagel. Jobu finally confesses her true feelings about how she see’s life: as a “swirling bucket of bullshit.” Jobu tells Evelyn that succumbing to the Bagel is the only way anyone can know the truth and that dark truth is what can finally set us free. Jobu has pushed too far by calling her mother Evelyn, at which point Evelyn finds a way to fight back: pinky-fu. Evelyn declares loudly for stop calling her by her name because, “I. Am. Your. Mother!” Evelyn finally has a hold of Jobu who in that moment decides to leave all the ‘verses behind and allow herself to fall into the Everything Bagel. Evelyn pulls on Jobu, trying to stop her from the destruction she’s subjecting herself to. But just as Evelyn said to the chef earlier, “no one is really alone.” Her family and the Alpha Jumpers all come to Evelyn’s aid and begin pulling on one another in a long chain. For as quick as this shot is, it holds a lot of significance: Every action Evelyn has taken to show love, care, and compassion to those who were attacking her brings them all together. The chain of Evelyn’s actions is reflected by the physical chain pulling on Jobu. They’re working together physically, and metaphorically Evelyn’s past experiences with each of the Jumpers and her family members has given her the strength to work towards helping Jobu become another version of herself. But Evelyn isn’t yet done helping Jobu evolve into a new kind of Joy.

  Suddenly we see Evelyn and Joy running from the laundromat into the parking lot, Joy screaming for her mother to stop. Joy takes a stand in front of the family business, her parents, and everyone inside and tells Evelyn that it’s great she’s learning about herself and growing but that Joy doesn’t, “...want to hurt anymore. And for some reason when I’m with you it hurts both of us. So, let’s just go our separate ways okay? Just let me go.” Joy wants a chance to live life on her terms and do things her way. She’s seeing now that it’s possible to change thanks to the example being set by her mother, even if it’s more overbearing and pushy than usual. Evelyn is going through the phase of her Awakening that inspires the newly Awakened to try to get everyone to see things as they do, to know what they know. The consequences of that in this case is for Joy to push back harder still. Instead of putting up another fight or trying to appeal to her daughter, Evelyn decides it’s time to do as Joy says: just let go.

  Joy has demanded space by putting up a boundary and Evelyn honors it. This is a huge step in the relationship that we’ve seen between these two throughout the movie. Flashes of other ‘verses flit by, showing Evelyn let go of Jobu in the [what verse?] ‘verse, showing Rock Joy falling over the edge of the cliff in front of Rock Evelyn. In the [what verse?] ‘verse, Jobu slowly backs into the Everything Bagel as it rotates violently atop the staircase. Jobu’s head slowly starts to fade into the open portal as Evelynt steps back and watches as what’s left of her daughter fades into the blackness of the Bagel, letting her child follow her own path and find her own way. Evelyn in every ‘verse is learning what it takes to be a good parent to an adult child is to let them be who they are. Evelyn has truly let go. A single tear slides down Evelynt’s cheek but then she turns back and we see that in the laundromat parking lot, Evelyn has decided to make one last appeal to her daughter.

  What’s happening here is a battle between mindsets:systems. This is a battle of programming. As a mother, Evelyn naturally only wants what is best for her child but Joy 

wants to do what’s best for herself outside of the systems she’s familiar with. Joy feels it’s time to move on and be the version of herself outside of her parent’s influence that she always wished to be. In order for that to happen for Joy, she has to take a step back from her family and explore life beyond them. Evelyn has learned so many new ways of being and has trouble being the parent that Joy needs her to be, that Joy deserves. As we’ll see later in Evelyn’s last big moment with her daughter in the film, there’s more yet both can learn from each other which sets the family up for future greatness thanks to a bit of metaprogramming.
