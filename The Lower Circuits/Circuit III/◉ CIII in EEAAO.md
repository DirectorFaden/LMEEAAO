In fact, let’s define Jobu Tupaki’s Area of Strength:Weakness straight away. I feel it’s clear that Jobu is strongly in an Area of Hostile Strength. While her physical presence itself isn’t the most intimidating because of her stature she still harbors a great deal of aggressive power that she willingly pushes outward onto others with. The first time we truly see Jobu come into her power is in her battle in the hallway of the IRS building. She exits the elevator in full Elvis-esque jumpsuit regalia, a leashed pig by her side. The cop scoffs at her, not taking Jobu seriously as a threat. The way Jobu asserts herself against the cop (“Is it that I can’t be here, or are you not allowing me to be here?”) shows us her clear disdain for authority. Solidified earlier by αWaymond when he’s explaining Jobu’s origins to Evelynt, “...now, she’s seen too much. Lost any sense of morality, any belief in objective truth.” Jobu Tupaki is the ultimate example of someone locked into the Area of Hostile Strength. I don’t believe Jobu is capable of being more well rounded, being able to jump between areas of Friendliness or Hostility. Despite this, Joyt still is. And in that underlying truth, there is hope.

For αWaymond, hope is what he stands by even in the face of almost assured destruction with the threat of Jobu Tupaki dangling in front of him and the entire Alphaverse. He’s strong, intelligent, quick witted, capable of great things in every sense. In my estimation, αWaymond lives mainly in the Area of Friendly Strength. His approach from the beginning with Evelynt is one of assertion when he’s helping her get in tune with the Alphaverse in the IRS elevator, donut umbrella at the ready. Even if he is forceful, he’s acting out of necessity. We don’t know it yet, but αWaymond has done this routine many, many times. His routine is one of assuming that every Evelyn he meets could possibly be the one to help stop Jobu Tupaki. He’s brute forcing each version of Evelyn into higher circuitry with his synchronization routine. He assumes that every Evelyn is “OK” in the sense that they won’t be outright hostile to him (maybe only out of confusion at the unfolding situation that’s new to them, of course). His intentions are pure, meant to help save every version of every person in the multiverse.

There is a major detriment to αWaymond being this way, however. RAW explains, “He or she [the Friendly Strength type] is satisfied with his or her life, and so is the rest of society. Alas, they nonetheless can get to the position where they need therapy of some sort, simply because they may take on too much responsibility and carry too many burdens.” As far as we know, the only active members of a resistance against Jobu Tupaki are αWaymond and the crew of the RV in the Alphaverse as they drive around in an attempt to keep Jobu at bay. All of these people are under a great deal of stress but are acting for a noble cause. And because of this stress, and the burden they bear in trying to save the multiverse, they can be hard on themselves and those around them. αWaymond may be the best version of Waymond, but he’s not free of Waymond’s way of being in the slightest.

With a basic understanding of how the Wang’s operate in these Strength:Weakness types as well as what it means to time bind, we can see how each member of the family works with time binding.

Obviously, Evelynt has the most trouble with time binding. Initially, Evelynt doesn’t have any real sense of time. She’s constantly trying to micromanage every aspect of not only her life but the lives of all her immediate family members. Her constant movement and strained focus also strains her ability to present and bind herself to her immediately happening timeline. She doesn’t realize how much she’s impacting the lives of those around her when she makes decisions that she thinks are only for herself. Doing the taxes effects the entire family because it allows them to time bind to their home which is a part of their CI needs. Since Evelynt doesn’t have enough sense of the present (her constant default state being CI), she can’t internalize her actions as something that can change her entire family’s sense of safety. She jeopardizes the family in the same way she jeopardizes herself. She doesn’t know any other way.

From what I can see, Waymondt seems very present and aware. By being able to manage his own needs, Evelynt’s needs, the needs of the laundromat, and STILL have time to interact with customers in a personable and signature silly way he must be in CIV. We can infer that the way he manages his lower circuitry is also by means of distraction. The thing is, not only is it a means of self-regulation but his actions radiate outward to those around him. When Waymondt acts and dances with Rick (the man who complains his $20 got eaten by the change machine), Waymondt is helping others the way he helps himself. You’ll see in CV how:why this works as a means of co-regulation.

Joyt is in such an emotional space when first entering the laundromat with Becky that she can’t focus on the present at all. When Becky barrages Joy with cheek kisses, it’s not just a way to show Joy love but also to get her back to focus on the task at hand. Joy seems to suddenly snap back and giggle her way into the reality in front of her. Joy’s emotional state (this is a hint! can you guess her circuit?) in even having to make time with her family causes her great distress. Joyt is not in her home territory and has a long running history of arguing with Evelynt. There’s no sense of safety coming home:to the laundromat. Joyt is deep in CII.

Once we meet Gong Gongt, with our description of him from Evelynt to color our expectations of him, we see he’s a very demanding and traditional man. His personality is one of patriarch. Gong Gongt wants his needs met immediately. He demands and doesn’t ask. Because of his nature, Evelynt’s circuitry has no chance of repair or change. He does take interest in speaking with Joyt but his disappointment in Joyt not keeping up with her Chinese language practice colors his impression of Evelynt’s family. Evelyn is not impressing him with how she has raised:taken care of her family. She’s fallen short of her responsibilities to them as much as she has failed herself. In this way, Gong Gongt is a mainly focused in CII but in the opposite way as Joyt. Where Joyt is the emotional part of CII, Gong Gongt is the territorial. He has taken on a leadership role. Leading to him means being able to uphold the territorial demands of the family. Seeing Evelynt fall short solidifies his stance to himself and only causes him greater distress. With Gong Gongt as the main example of what it means to manage oneself in a familial role, Evelynt not being able to raise herself out of CI makes sense.

A major part of Jobu Tupaki’s power is her ability to change her time binding at will. Initially trying to understand how she jumps from one timeline in the Thetaverse to the next is difficult. The first exposure we have to it is when Evelynt dies in the broom closet after talking to αWaymond for this first time in the “burner universe.” Seeing each version of Evelyn die in rapid succession as Jobu jumps from one ‘verse to the next we get a visually understanding of time binding and how it works for her. Jobu has found a way to exist beyond time. In part because she is without having a permanent reality she wants to bind to because of her pursuit of every Evelyn everywhere, Jobu becomes the ultimate version of herself. Life beyond time means a life beyond morals, no need to practice her sociability (notice the way she completely ignores Becky when she leaves the bar?), and her singular focus on killing the Evelyns becomes the way she judges her reality. Ironically, this isn’t much different from Joyt who ALSO judges her reality based on how she relates to Evelynt. The common thread that bind Joyt and Evelynt is a need for acceptance from family members who have no interest in accepting their lifestyle.

Time binding becomes a means by which we as circuit users judge our own reality as well. The circumstances of the lives we’ve built to exist in as well as the pressures on our lives from outside circumstances beyond our control creates the circumstances by which our ability to time bind is effected. None of us are free from the feelings that come from our ability to regulate our time binding. There’s a reason meditation focuses so much on the act of remaining present. Meditation is the active practice of time binding in all systems of the body simultaneously. For people new to a meditation practice, finding guided meditations is a means of practicing binding to these different systems. Limiting initial engagement with meditation is also an important part of this practice. Because the new practitioner doesn’t fully understand what it means to release the pressures that are upon them, they often feel a great deal of strain and discomfort in the practice. Allowing their whole self to acclimate to a new way of being takes time. Making the most of a mindful practice of any kind that the practitioner may find relief for mind:body:beyond takes time. And in regaining a sense of time that’s meaningful to them, the practitioner finds the practice engaging, soothing, and hopefully an integral part of living their best lives.
