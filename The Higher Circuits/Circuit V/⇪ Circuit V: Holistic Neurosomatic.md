Based on how science currently understands the ANS through polyvagal theory, my difficulty with processing emotions and experience makes sense. 

According to polyvagal theory, there are three states the parasympathetic nervous system (part of the ANS) can move between based on external stimulus. There’s the ventral vagal state, active when the ANS is in a state of safety or homeostasis. When someone is in this state, they tend to be more socially connected and present, along with their unconscious bodily functions behaving in their individually expected ways: blood flows easily, breathing is natural, digestion is fully functional.

In a sympathetic state, the body readies itself for response. Fight, flight, and fawn responses are all part of the sympathetic vagal response. Major changes happen in the anatomy: digestion shuts down, blood flow to major muscle groups increases, adrenaline is released. Socially, the only way someone in sympathetic response can relate to others is based entirely on whether another person is perceived as a threat or potential threat. There’s little to no chance of social connection. The fawn response is a last ditch effort for the sympathetic person to save themselves using a social context before resorting to fight or flight.

The dorsal vagal state is a complete shutdown of any other systems of response. Socially, a person in this state is mentally preparing for death. They might dissociate, become hopeless or depressed, they might even experience a total shutdown of mobility thus why it’s called the freeze state. Both body and mind are locked down.

And getting to the foundation of this Circuit, to be holistic is to exist as a whole, and whole things that have interconnected parts that exist outside of that whole are gestalts. First, some definitions.

Things that are holistic are understood to be individual parts that are interconnected, and those parts make up a larger whole. Awareness of those interconnected parts acting as one whole and explaining how the parts work together is what makes something holistic. Presumably you, the reader, are a human being. Guess what? That by default makes you a holistic system. You are composed of nothing but interconnected parts that make you a whole, and you are understood:perceived as one thing.

Holistic practices then are nothing spiritual as much as they are grounded in the physical reality of the human body and how it acts as a whole. 

When people bring feelings of spirituality to their holistic practices it’s a personal choice. There’s no need to be spiritual:religious to act holistically. Considering how grounded in physical reality our definition of holistic is, you can be whoever you are right now and be holistic.

When something is a gestalt, it is understood to be greater than the sum of its parts. i love the example of seeing a jigsaw puzzle as a gestalt. We know going in to a jigsaw puzzle that we’re trying to recreate a whole picture. The picture is composed of individual pieces that are cut in specific ways to fit back together and show you the whole picture. We, the puzzle solvers, are trying to find ways to relate all of these separate pieces in different ways so we can put the puzzle together again and see the whole picture. We make connections using these individual pieces while keeping the greater whole in mind so that we can understand how meaningful the relationships between the pieces are.
