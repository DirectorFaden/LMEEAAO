As we learned earlier, the Eight Circuits stack on top of one another as awareness moves from one Circuit to the next. Every Circuit serves a purpose with several having multiple functions. The best way to learn what each Circuit has felt like for me has been to develop a meditation that lets me rest in each one, one by one. Every Circuit has its own feeling, i’ve found. They resonate through my mind and body in different ways, and cause me to react to them differently as well.

The best way to do this meditation is to do it in whatever way my body is comfortable at that moment. i might be sitting, lying down, standing…it doesn’t matter. What matters is my physical comfort comes first, which directly ties into Circuit I. Once i’m in a position that i can rest in for a period, i begin my meditation with two deep, full breaths. In through the nose deeply, and out through the mouth slowly and completely. i also focus on making the out breath longer than the in breath. The goal is to make it about twice as long, but i don’t stress about it if i don’t do that. 

Circuit I: Oral Bio-Survival resides mainly in the brainstem and involuntary nervous system. i start by moving all of my attention to the brainstem and keep a steady pace with my breath through my nose, in and out. Once i feel comfortable here, i take the name of the Circuit literally: first i focus on my mouth and everything connected to it. My mouth will tend to salivate a bit when i first do this. Afterwards i think about my stomach. Am i fed? Have i eaten enough to be satiated? If not, is it something i can fix after i meditate? If i have eaten, what did i eat? Does what i’ve eaten make my body feel good? 

After this check in with around mouth, stomach, and food, i move on to the greater Bio-Survival questions. i’ll ask things like: do i feel safe where my body is now? Am i in any immediate danger here? If for some reason i don’t feel safe, i open my eyes and check my environment. This helps me establish the outside world and assess if what i feel is true or not. If i don’t feel safe, i move immediately. The only way self-regulation works with the Eight Circuits is if every need in Circuit I is met. There’s no way around this. Once i establish physical safety, i move on to body temperature. Do i need less clothing or more to regulate that? Should i put a fan on? As i move through this long checklist i continue to focus my attention where this Circuit resides. After all of this is done i begin moving my attention slightly upward and towards the left hemisphere of the brain to start meditating with…

Circuit II: Anal Emotional Territorial which works with the hindbrain, the voluntary nervous system, and the muscles. The cerebellum, pons, midbrain, and thalamus are all active within Circuit II. Before you even ask, yes, i do actually check on the state of my anus before moving on to the more brain and mind based parts of this Circuit. Doing this is a great way to find out more about my current emotional state. If i’m tightening myself, i might be stressed or uneasy. If it’s comfortable, i likely don’t have anything to worry about. i even ask when the last time i had a bowel movement was. There’s no shame in checking in with every part of my self. That’s what this meditation is ultimately about. Plus, consider where a lot of nervous or anxious feelings tend to show up for people: the stomach. If the stomach is in distress, it can lead to poor bowel function.

Emotions of all kinds are under consideration in Circuit II. One could argue that emotions are a reflection of the way one experiences their environment:territory. Territory can mean a lot of different places depending on the scale. The immediate environment around someone at any given time is a territory. Is it public or private? Is it intimate or crowded? What about where i work, what’s it like to be in that territory? Or at home? Does my home feel like a safe place to be? How comfortable am i here? That’s ultimately the question i ask myself when meditating in Circuit II: how acclimated am i to my environment? Any challenges to my comfort or ease are something i pay particular mind to. If there’s anything around me i can change within the next few minutes that can raise my comfort level, i do it. Sometimes nothing can be done about particular environmental challenges, and i give myself a moment to recognize it and let that element of the space be as it is. It’s not always possible to change things immediately. Recognizing that is a vital step in finding a path towards comfort.

Circuit III: Time-Binding Semantic Rational has me moving my attention further forward in the left hemisphere of my brain to the left cortex. There is a lot to be mindful of in this Circuit, but the most pressing part during meditation is the time-binding aspect. What i pay attention to, how long i pay attention to it, and whether i get lost in thought are all part of the way i analyze CIII. It’s far too easy for me to get lost thinking of the past or imagining the future at any given time in my life. When there’s nothing left to be aware of visually there’s nothing to stop my brain from leaning in to time travelling. Grounding techniques such as listening intently to environmental noises can lead to clearer mental space and bring me back to the present. i do my best to remain present for an extended period of time before i move on to Circuit IV. Semantics and rationality are quite important in everyday life, but since i’m not communicating with anyone outside of my self during meditation they aren’t an immediate concern. 

Circuit IV: “Moral” Social Sexual moves my attention further forward still to the left neocortex. To meditate on my “morality” doesn’t take much. Mostly i ask if i find myself still in alignment with the way i want to exist in the world. As long as i haven’t done anything cruel, hurtful, or purposefully mean to my self or others i’m happy with how i’ve moved through my day to that point. Sexuality is something i don’t often consider in my daily life. One thing that hasn’t changed for me is how little awareness around my sexual presence i have. Whether or not i have challenged someone sexually, accidentally flirted with someone, or have been seen as attractive never crosses my mind. i can see other people around me as attractive, but since the meditation is meant to focus on how i’m feeling residing in each Circuit, none of these aforementioned things are an issue. 

What can be difficult to get through however is the social aspect of Circuit IV. i have been called charming by quite a few people over the last year plus, but i don’t personally understand where that’s coming from. i don’t consider myself to be particularly socially adept. Especially when it comes to people i admire or am intimidated by, i lock up and lack the social niceties that i have been praised for by others. This is something i still struggle with a great deal, and i’m not entirely sure how to remedy it for myself. Regardless, meditation in this Circuit means being honest with how i feel i’ve been around others and how i feel i’ve been interacting with them on that day. My social aspect is consistently one of the most variable parts of my self from one day to the next.

Third Eye Bridge – While the Circuits themselves don’t call on the frontal lobe directly, i tend to place my awareness at the “third eye”, that small space just above where the bridge of the nose begins and where eyebrows are separate in the center of my forehead, as part of my meditation routine. Here i find myself in a space where i can reassure that all of the Lower Circuits have been checked and accounted for. Moving into the Higher Circuits requires a certain level of spiritual understanding or physical holistic awareness due to those being the function of Circuit V. Sometimes i can feel that spiritual presence in my self resting on the surface of my awareness. When it’s easily accessed, i have no issues taking that spiritual feeling with me and shifting into the Higher Circuit spaces. 

When i struggle to feel holistically or spiritually aware, i remind myself that it’s not a place that i try to reach as much as it is something i make space for within me. i’ve never been able to force myself into that spiritual headspace successfully, Historically it feels like i’m trying to punch through a brick wall with my fists, trying to brute force my way into feeling whole. What can be done if i’m struggling to feel my way into Circuit V though is to quietly, to myself, imagine a small sexual thrill i have either experienced before or long for in that moment. RAW mentions the connection between the sexual and the spiritual in his Circuit V. Specifically he says that sexual experiences can easily become transcendental if one allows themselves to experience the whole of their sexual play without reservations. I’ve found that this “small thrill” technique has helped me refocus my efforts and find Circuit V consistently and relatively easily. It’s important to note that my focus does not remain on the “small thrill” i choose for myself as that would be a function of Circuit IV which is not the goal. It’s a quick detour to sexual presence and then back on the road to holism.

Circuit V: Holistic Neurosomatic, as i’ve mentioned, is focused on the connection between brain and body. Remember: neuro- as in neuron, the synapses that create connections in the physical brain and communicate with the CNS, and soma- as in body, the physical vessel which one occupies, are very real components of who i am in the world. The reason spirituality is part of the whole that is me is because that revelation of spirit is what links the ephemeral to the tangible. Truly understanding who i am as a person in the world took building a sense of spiritual connection to things outside of myself but that i internalized to make the physical whole of me be a complete picture i experience through interactions with other real-world objects. 

While meditating in this space, what i focus on is how clearly i can feel my physical feelings. i focus my attention on the right hemisphere of my brain and allow any sensations resonate throughout my body, wherever they may go. Often it is a matter of movement through the sensation of my nerves. i can sometimes feel how the feelings and sensations move through my CNS very clearly. This can sometimes cause an “all over” effect where my whole body and brain feel in a similar excited state of activation, nerve endings and neurons firing in a complimentary way with one another. i find that reaching this state requires having deep, steady breath control. Creating the space in myself and experiencing these feelings is one of the more gratifying parts of the meditation for me. It opens me up to even greater experiences to come.

Circuit VI: Collective Neurogenetic resides in the DNA, RNA, and specifically the right neocortex of the brain. Meditating in this Circuit means remaining aware of the holistic self i’ve just gotten in touch with but also attempting to expand that feeling of awareness beyond the physical boundaries of my body. The best way i can describe this is that my awareness begins to trace the outline of my physical self and, once traced, i try to expand that outline beyond the lines of my body. My eyes remain closed and i try to imagine how much of my immediate environment i can “bring into” the feeling of my physical self. As this Circuit is focused on interconnectivity, the more i am able to connect to everything around me, living and non-living alike, the more connected i am to the whole of the room i’m in, the building i’m in, the town i’m in, and so on. The holistic becomes gestalt then holistic again as my physical boundaries expand well beyond the physical boundaries of my body.

What i’m checking for in this Circuit as i meditate is how expansive my awareness can become. Expanding my body beyond its bounds helps to expand my mind to see in ways it may have lost some awareness of. The more often i expand my awareness, the more often i find how easy it is to keep my ego in check, to keep my goals for life in sight, and to move in ways that benefit others as much as they benefit me. Staying present within Circuit VI means challenging any sense of individualism i have and attempting to shrink my ego. Remaining present in the collective means i consider myself both part of and acting as if i am all things, together, acting as one. Each part of a collective realizes they are part of it. Actions taken by one entity affect many others. There are no actions that can be taken that don’t affect the objects nearby. Once i reach the limits of my expansion i try to rest in that feeling:space for as long as i am able, but without trying to hold onto it too tightly. The longer i can remain in that space, the better i feel after i leave it. i also feel better prepared for...

Circuit VII: Meta-Programming – The function of this circuit is to train one's brain into changing something. The physical location of Circuit VII is in the frontal lobes of the brain. All major executive functions of the brain take place here: focused attention, critical thinking skills, building and executing plans, self-control, and many more. CVII’s location and vast skill set gives it the ability to reach and engage with every other circuit as it operates. The idea of CVII as part of my meditation routine was difficult to figure out for me at first. How is it that i might concentrate on the combination of brain:mind:body without directly engaging with it? What does it look:feel like to make space in my mind for my mind? Just how meta- can i make my practice? The answer: infinitely. This isn’t ideal for remaining present enough in meditation to make the practice seem worthwhile. It’s far too easy to get lost exploring the depths of my selves during this part of meditation. Instead of trying to look too deeply inward, once i reach CVII i instead get into a similar place to CVI except i add my awareness to the expansive feeling i’ve built. This has consistently brought the best results since moving from CVI to CVII takes much less effort with adding awareness than any other way i’ve tried.

Circuit VIII: Non-Local Quantum – Located throughout “the whole system”, the final Circuit in my meditation is the most challenging of all to remain in. If meditation in CVI brings my mind to share interconnectedness with all things, and CVII adds in my awareness of being interconnected, then meditating in CVIII means experiencing both my awareness of interconnection and the experience of it in concert with purposely trying to place my “whole system” within quantum reality instead of classical reality. Where with CVI and CVII i’m trying to expand my awareness outward, in CVIII i’m concentrating on all of the smallest parts of my self and what i’m connected to. Experiencing quantum reality in a coherent, understandable way is nearly impossible. i’ve only ever had fleeting moments of feeling quantum reality this way. What i have experienced however keeps me coming back for more. For me, the feelings i get when i do have a CVIII experience are akin to being freshly Awakened. i cannot think of any other comparison. My ego is drastically reduced, my feelings of the world around me are enhanced, my perception is sharp, my mind racing with ideas, my heart full of joy.

Though i keep chasing after that feeling, it’s important i always remember that experiencing that feeling is not the ultimate goal of this meditation. Chasing a high doesn’t work in the long run. It only ever serves to make one feel dejected and low in spirit. Having the full experience of CVIII is the same way. i learned, early in my practice, that trying to always be in this state practically guarantees it will rarely come. Much like the quantum science i’m basing this entire system on, expecting to measure a specific result changes the result. 

Whenever i feel i’m ready to end my meditation, i try to do it slowly. My intention is to bring myself down to CIV or CV before opening my eyes. Best laid plans, etc. Sometimes i’ll have set a timer and can’t bring my awareness down. Other times, circumstances beyond my control jolt me from my concentration. Still other times i might simply be too tired to continue and bring myself back to the present. However i end my practice, the results are nearly always positive. i often find myself in a better mood than when i started, have clearer presence of mind, and feel more energetic. i love putting in the work to make myself feel better, especially when all i need to do is relax my body and concentrate on my mind.
