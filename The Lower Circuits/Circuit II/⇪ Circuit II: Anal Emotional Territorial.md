**Circuit II: Anal Emotional Territorial**

---

**Physical Location** - Thalamus, Voluntary Nervous System, Muscles

**Functions** - Group social dynamics

**Relation to Time** - Time as dictated by the group

Let’s backtrack a moment. Evelyn’s life in the laundromat isn’t only run by her inability to move out of Circuit I most of the time. Her life is always informed by her constant panic, that’s true. But in order for her to assert her place as the Gifted Child:person in charge, her (limited) ability to move into Circuit II keeps her in that position of power.

Circuit II is “concerned entirely with power politics” and “centralized in the thalamus – the ‘back brain’ or ‘old brain’ and is linked with the voluntary nervous system and the muscles,” RAW proposes. For clarity: i will continue to use the term “hindbrain” to avoid any confusion as these three terms are interchangeable. Also the thalamus is located above the midbrain, in a central location within the brain itself.

The sections of the brain RAW asserts Circuit II resides in are mainly concerned with physical control. Our individual abilities to control our bodies the way we want is highly individual based on our personal health. In general, the voluntary nervous system runs our mental signals for movements through alpha motor neurons in the spinal cord out towards the muscles that are being told to move. Alpha motor neurons run the incoming signals from many places in the body, including the thalamus. Signals move down the spinal cord to its exit at the ventral root in our core, and then follow the appropriate nerves to the muscles we want to use. 

The thalamus itself acts as a direct link to the neocortex, the largest part of the brain which is divided into multiple lobes. The thalamus processes then relays all sensory information except olfactory (smell) information and sends that information to the appropriate areas of the brain. Since the thalamus is linked to the majority of the brain this way, it can quickly relay information that comes in so that muscles can be triggered by the voluntary nervous system for use. 

Essentially, RAW believes the idea of trying to challenge those in power and become someone who themselves has power to exert over others begins as we enter the toddler stage of life. A toddler learning to stand, then walk, is itself an assertive act. It implies the toddler is attempting to conquer its environment by using its body. Once they’re able to walk and talk (crudely at first for both, of course) a toddler will begin to look towards those who exert force on them and see if they’re able to take that control for themselves.

This development is the birth of the ego. The toddler is learning what its place is among its family members. Depending on the sort of nurturing they receive, and their own inherent “nature” (demeanors:how they act as a person outside of the influence of others), they may find themselves in roles of dominance or submission. How this goes is highly dependent on how Circuit I has been developed. RAW explains: where Circuit I “is chiefly imprinted by the mother, the second…circuit is chiefly imprinted by the father – the nearest alpha male.”

I cannot, on principle, agree with this idea in totality. In a standard expectation of the outdated system of “nuclear family,” perhaps it was more true. Whatever the case, whoever may be in charge of a child as they grow into the toddler stage is who the toddler will model themselves after and challenge. And whoever it is that responds to the toddler determines the relationship dynamic based on their own ability:inability to control their circuits and behaviors.

Territorial conflicts arise on both the nursery room floor and the world stage. The scale of the conflicts are obviously proportionally different, but the logic behind them is the same. “I’m the one in charge here, this whole place is mine! You have to do what I say when I say it!” The toddler and the caregiver:the competing militaries are at odds over control of the environment. Here’s where the “Anal” part of Circuit II comes in.

Have you ever noticed that when someone is belittling another person, most of the phrases they use to do it reference the anus? “You piece of shit!” “Shithead!” “Asshole!” When people lie to us, we tell them they’re “full of shit.” These phrases and so many others are thrown about with abandon. We equate the function of our bowels with a lower status. Things that are “shit” are beneath us. We make sure not to step in shit so that we don’t carry it with us. “Dealing with all of that bullshit” is a “huge pain in the ass.”

Ever wonder why we talk this way? When it comes to emotionally-charged territorial reactions, we often react based on our “gut” instinct, which most of us are told to trust from a young age. In order to deal with an “asshole” we take in sensory information about them, we assess it, and then voluntarily react based on the outcome we’re trying to achieve against them. The signal moves from hindbrain to thalamus near the midbrain, out to the appropriate areas of the brain that are being signaled for use, traveling through the alpha motor neurons down the spine, and then out to any muscles that need to move. Use of Circuit II is complete.

Our “gut” processes all of the food we put into it, takes out the good, and gets rid of the bad. When we deal with matters of territory and personal safety, we get to voluntarily react the same way. If we feel safe around someone, we can give them the opportunity to prove why they’re safe and accept them into our territory. If we have a strong negative reaction to someone, it’s time to get rid of that “piece of shit.” We push them away from us by whatever means necessary, if we have that inclination. Remember that we’re always taking in signals from our environment and the other people in it. Each person’s sense of safety is entirely based on their surrounding signals and how they’ve learned to interpret them.

Before moving on, i think it best to start discussing ideas of robotization put forward by RAW in PR here. Throughout this work, RAW defines those who are bound to the “lower circuits” I-IV have been programmed into being that way. There are several causes for robotization: society, family, the family’s social status, the expectations of a person, their environment…basically anything that can make an impact on the way someone behaves can lead to robotization.

When we think of a robot, we think of what it’s programmed to do. i always think immediately of Issac Asimov’s Laws of Robotics. “A robot may not injure a human or, through inaction, allow a human being to come to harm. A robot must obey orders given it by human beings except where such orders would conflict with the First Law. A robot must protect its own existence as long as such protection does not conflict with the First or Second Law.” This program introduces a foundation for robotic action that can be built upon.

When it comes to humans, programming isn’t as straightforward. Our brains are not computers. Though that’s true, we can use the model of a computer to compare with the brain. We can learn how a brain operates in Circuits, programs, and how using those two models can allow us to change our own programming. RAW explains, “When we say A is B, we are saying that A is only what it appears within our field of study or our area of specialization. This is saying too much.” There’s a huge difference between saying something “is” another thing and comparing something to another thing.

The physical parts of a computer are called hardware. Your laptop, your phone, the physical parts that comprise your PC, all of that is a physical component of a computer. Software are the programs installed on the computer that make it function the way you want them to. From Solitaire to Excel, everything a computer does is a function of software.

Comparing that to people, our hardware are our bodies and everything that physically exists inside of them. The brain, organs, nerves, are like the hardware of a computer. The parts exist to serve a function, and often these parts work together. They only work based on the software installed in the mind, though. The mind runs programs based on information input. The programs exist in this nebulous space of the mind after being input. Whether that input comes from lived experience, interactions with other people, personal thoughts, movies watched, or words on a page, all of that information once processed is then a function of software, of programming.

The consciousness of a person is much more complicated than that, though. Remember that what the Thinker thinks, the Prover proves. As long as something is physically possible within your universe, you can think about it and prove whatever it is you want about it based on the functions of programming. 

RAW explains the four basic parts of programming using the following terms (with my definitions that follow):

Genetic Imperatives - The instinctual parts of the brain that react based on need. To move toward safety or away from harm. See: Circuit I.
Imprints - The human brain is open to imprinting during certain times in development based on genetic designs. Going from the infant to toddler stage is one such time of imprint vulnerability. The programs installed during these stages can act like genetic imperatives once installed. Imprints set the limits of impactfulness Conditioning and Learning have, because Imprints create a solidified “form” in which programs can “function”. 
Conditioning - As imprints are installed, they can then be programmed further. Conditioning is a function of repeated actions taken over time to change the functions of imprints, often by force. Conditioning can come from external or internal sources.
Learning - Like conditioning but often less forceful. Usually comes from an internal want:need to take in information.

All four parts of programming then create a “map” for our brains to follow based on our experiences. This is what makes up our experience of reality. The Thinker thinks about input, the Prover, now with a form:map to follow, fits all incoming experience to within the form:map limits, making sure not to exceed them.

Now that we have a better understanding of programming, what is the function:purpose of robotization? At its core, robotization allows for a person to function within the confines of the society they inhabit. People are taught from birth how to be good citizens, to take pride in their country, obey the country’s laws, and in capitalist societies to SPENDCONSUME. Based on these imprints, people’s behavior isn’t always the core of who they are. Quite the opposite in fact, we see that who they are is hidden behind a layer of servitude to their systems of control, to what they were programmed for.

Where Circuit I is bound to the most basic functional needs of a person and their ability to meet those needs, Circuit II is where we meet assertion of power by force. Namely, force can be exerted through body language, violence, and other physical consequences. Conditioning people into systems is a function of institutional power, and the threat of Circuit II actions of force can be enough to keep those without power from challenging that power.

Even if you’ve never had one yourself, think of a time when you saw a toddler acting out. They often start by trying to use their limited vocabulary. “But mommy, i want it!” When their needs are denied, the toddler starts to cry, to scream, to throw their body around, to pound fists at the caregiver(s), and otherwise try to make the most of their limited size that they might gain some kind of advantage:acquiescence from the one(s) in charge. If the caregiver has strong Circuit II functioning and are seen by the toddler as a powerful figure, such acting out is punished. Otherwise a caregiver with weak Circuit II functioning that gives in to a demanding child shifts the balance of power. This shift begins to confuse the relationship between the child and the caregiver. A power struggle is born.

PR doesn’t delve far enough into the area of social hierarchy in this unit for my satisfaction, so i’m adding an addendum. RAW briefly mentions this struggle between toddler and caregiver in that the sudden abilities to both walk and talk lead to challenging the social order. But the order is one that’s asserted by territorial dominance enhanced by familial politics. The often upheld ideal is that the caregiver(s) are in charge of the family and whoever is under them does as they’re told. The stability of this unit depends fully on the caregivers’ ability to command others with the implication that they are fully capable of commanding themselves. Without this demonstration of self-regulation, those under the caregiver’s oversight are destined to either challenge the social order or try to self-regulate using improper means invented either by their internal guidance or by the influence of other people outside of them (and this can be from real people in their lives or from media sources consumed).

The Circuit II actions of all human beings are built by myriad influences around them as they grow, programmed into them, and then any input after that programming is accepted fits the parameters of the program map. Orders of dominance and submission are a vital part of someone understanding their place in a family:work environment:military: the world. RAW gives an example of how dominance:submission works on a two-dimensional grid that uses Circuit I and Circuit II as archetypes. Advance and retreat work for and against dominance and submission. The four spaces on the grid are as follows:

Area of Hostile Strength – “I’m OK, you’re not OK”
Area of Friendly Strength – “I’m OK, you’re OK”
Area of Hostile Weakness – “I’m not OK, you’re not OK”
Area of Friendly Weakness – “I’m not OK, you’re OK”

From these oversimplified archetypes (and RAW assures us they are vastly oversimplified if only for convenience of getting across greater ideas later) we see some basic ways people live and act in the world emerge. 

People who dominantly imprint Hostile Strength are as the name implies: these are the people who, most often, are so concerned with asserting their dominance over others that they concentrate their focus on the physical enhancement of their bodies. They get fit not for health reasons as much as for their need to show off how dominant they are on sight. They grow their bodies that they might find solace in their minds, and be seen as someone worth admiring:fearing:obeying unquestionably. Anyone this type perceives as lesser is “not OK,” meaning they are someone the Hostile Strength practitioner can dominate in some fashion and exert control over. 

Those who take on Friendly Strength find that dignity, equity, equality, and understanding exerts a sense of leadership naturally without having to resort to games of intimidation the way a Hostile Strength person would. Friendly Strength people are usually identified as “natural-born leaders,” or people who enjoy guiding others through life who might not be able to guide themselves. Kindness and generosity are two traits that Friendly Strength people put into the world without expending much of their energy. They feel good about feeling good themselves, and like when they can help other people feel good with them.

Hostile Weakness people are those that always hold resistance to any ideas:actions that are not already within their comfort zones. They’re ready to push back against things they don’t understand, and usually don’t have any intentions of trying to understand them. People like this are usually the ones who insist that things must change to anyone who will listen, but will NEVER take action to bring about that change themselves. They want so badly to be in control but don’t want any of the responsibility that comes with it. Their actions in life are most ruled by resentment and paranoia.

Finally there is Friendly Weakness. These people have been programmed in such a way as to not have any convictions:thoughts of their own. They are those that don’t seek anything but the easiest path through life. Whenever they see resistance, they run away from it or submit to it. Wherever they see a straight path ahead, they take it, often without thought of consequence or next steps. Friendly Weakness people think they don’t have to take responsibility for their actions (so they may think). They’re ready to give control of their lives to whoever wants it, because choosing their own path is too much for them to handle.

These are all broad generalizations, but these programs of strength and weakness allow us to see the general effects of robotization. Without being able to enact any critical thinking skills until (statistically speaking) between five and ten years old, most children become imprinted with these programs and, without serious introspection, may well get stuck in one. That said, it's not just children but all people, at any age, who are susceptible to these programs. Anyone can be made to fall into these categories given the proper circumstances. More often than not people get stuck in these programs for life, unaware that they’re even acting out in these ways thanks to the exertion of forces outside of themselves (though with awareness, not outside of their control).

RAW continues: “...whether or not you or i were born with a predisposition for one quadrant, all organisms are born with a predisposition for imprint vulnerability, and the imprint, once set in the neural circuitry, acts as robotically as any genetic hardwiring.” Most people never think to question the way they act. We are made to not have time to question ourselves, thanks to the survival stress most of us endure on a daily basis. How can you take time to think beyond your immediate needs if you’re always struggling? The problem of robotization is systemic and purposeful. You are programmed to be used. Once you’re no longer useful, you’re discarded. 

As dire as this sounds, we each have a chance to change our programming. In order for someone to change, they must accept that change is challenging. None of us can learn to grow unless we accept that growing is something we want. Then, we can take steps toward attaining that growth. Learning to act in ways antithetical to the ways we’ve been programmed is infinitely helpful. For instance, if someone is shy, they can practice speaking up. If they’re dominant, they might practice giving others a chance to have their voices heard. Over time, and with effort, people will ease into more situations that challenge their programming. As people challenge themselves, they become more balanced. They gain the ability to become the person needed most at any given moment in order to live a life guided by their core morality and principals. 

Balance comes from acting in ways that could be part of the other programs of strength or weakness that someone hasn’t primarily imprinted. That’s why self improvement is a challenge. People start acting outside of their programming, outside of their comfort zone. There’s plenty of room for overlap, nuance, and experimentation between these four types of programming. “Any system for describing human behavior should be flexible enough to be extended indefinitely, and should also still contain meaning when reduced back to its fundamentals.” In other words, within the structure of these four limiting examples of programming, there’s room for humanity. Be mindful of how i spoke of them: i never implied one person is entirely within one kind of robotization. There’s always overlap, and as such, there’s always an opportunity for growth.
