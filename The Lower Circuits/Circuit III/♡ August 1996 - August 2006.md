When it comes to body connectivity, i have struggled greatly. This struggle became more present as i got older. Starting at age 10, i found my ability to be present was faltering. At this point i had been on a steady dose of barbiturate from just before my toddler (CII) stage until exactly my 10th birthday. 

First thing that morning wasn’t a greeting or celebration, it was an awakening in the early dawn hours to get ready for a doctor’s appointment. What i remember most is how groggy and frightened i felt. My mom had been keeping me informed for weeks leading up to the day. She told me as much as she knew about MRI procedures. She let me know that it could be the end of my medication needs, and reassured me that whatever was going to happen that the rest of the day was going to be all about positive things for me. i felt somewhat at ease with Mom’s close attention. And so, we made our way.

At the location, i remember the moody lighting of the MRI room. The walls were a neutral tan but much of the walls and equipment were cast in shadow as though i were being prepared for sleep more than an active procedure. The scale of the MRI machine seemed massive to me. Here in the middle of this room was a hulking behemoth of technology placed in the center of a room that looked more at home in a mausoleum than any medical facility. Laying down on the moving platform felt like i was being put into a casket, my wildest vampiric fantasies coming to life around me and to me.

The procedure itself didn’t last very long. After weeks of preamble and preparation, ten minutes of lying still were all it took for doctors to see what the activity in my brain looked like. They assessed my results and declared that yes, i was to be weaned off of barbiturates slowly over the next month and then stop taking them for good. Whatever they saw was enough, and so was enough for my mom. We left, Mom in good spirits and myself somewhat confused. There was a part of me that was happy but i didn’t know what my life would be like without this medication. In terms of consciousness, living with the effects of this drug was all i had ever known. What would life be like without it? How would i feel:act? i did my best to cast these thoughts out of my mind and enjoy the rest of the day.

One of my favorite things to do as a child was going to the beach and swimming in the ocean. i had a few ways i liked to catch waves as well. Body boarding and boogie boarding became a natural extension of who i was when i was out at sea. Feeling the tide push in and out, the frothy waves lapping at my torso and back in calm repetition felt like home. So for my tenth birthday going to the beach was all i really wanted. Age 10 felt like a huge milestone for little me. There would never be another day in my life that i wasn’t a double digit age. i wanted to make the most of this momentous occasion and luckily my request was answered.

At this point, my parents had already been divorced and somewhat estranged for about three years. Seeing Dad meant monitored visitation time in neutral locations that were monitored by people assigned by the State. They were always short, awkward, and unsatisfying for (most likely) all involved. i didn’t like seeing my Dad in these short bursts but it was the only way i could at the time. But for my tenth birthday there was an exception. Along with Mom, Amanda, and myself, Dad joined us for my birthday at the beach.

Getting down onto the sand, my parents set up some big towels, a cooler full of snacks and one of drinks, a giant umbrella, and we all started to settle in. Amanda loved making sand castles so she, Dad, and me all spent some time digging out a moat and building our palace. Dad was great at making it so the tide washing onto the shore would fill the moat and wash back out. It made the entire build seem more permanent than ephemeral. Even after finishing and abandoning our build, Amanda and i would look back at it from time to time to see how much it had changed over time.

Eventually the call of the sea had to be answered. It’s splashy siren song was irresistible to me. i let my parents know what i was doing and then made my way into the waves like i had countless times before. As i stepped further into the waves, one foot after another, i let myself be enveloped by the power and the beauty of the shimmering water around me. What myself or my parents didn’t realize however was that it was mating season for rays. Those flat, bottom skating creatures with large flapping fins on either side of their strangely diamond shaped bodies, their sharp and brutal tails that they used to hunt prey and defend themselves, were closer to the surface of the sea than any of us knew. Stepping into the ocean always felt right to me. It was something i never questioned. Until this day.

i can’t remember exactly how far in to the ocean i got. It couldn’t have been more than fifteen feet. Without warning, i suddenly felt a powerful sting between my second and big toe on my right foot. At first i thought i had stepped on a seashell that was sitting at a strange angle in the sand beneath the water. That thought was quickly replaced by a burning sensation in my foot and then the pain that followed. Realizing i needed to get out of the water, i turned around as best i could with a hobbling walk that focused all of my steps with my right foot onto my heel. Once my parents noticed me approaching them, they knew something was wrong. Neither of them knew what to do, so my Dad found a lifeguard to help. I let the lifeguard what had happened and how i was feeling. He immediately knew both from my description and from looking at the wound that i had been stung by a ray. He helped carry me to a nearby bathroom on the beach and told me the only way to relieve the pain was for me to pee on my foot. The urea, a chemical in urine, was a quick and easily accessible way to clean, disinfect, and begin the healing process of a ray’s sting. Confused, in pain, my body a freshly included aspect into my conscious being, i dropped my swim trunks and did as i was told. i christened my new double digit life in those moments with my father and a stranger nearby, their backs turned but listening for me to finish. To that point it was the most humiliating moment of my life.

After that, it was time to leave the beach. i was too distressed to do anything else, and walking around too difficult. We got home, my Mom told the story to my Grandma, and we went on with cake and presents after a quick round of Neosporin and bandages. What a strange day. It started with a medical diagnosis, progressed into a minor medical emergency, and concluded as though nothing of either sort had happened. i couldn’t help but feel that the entire ordeal was a disaster and felt very distressed for the remainder of the day. Like the good kid i was expected to be, i thanked everyone for their gifts and we went home soon after. That night i lay in bed, these thoughts of a changing life and body ahead of me. i doubt i got any real rest that night. Too much had happened to not have my mind preoccupied with the spiraling suddenness of the changes being thrust upon me. My ability to be present was suddenly in question and already beginning to waver. Not knowing what was going to happen to me or what kind of person i would become was scary. Already in my life i had faced so much confusion, so many sudden changes in lifestyle and location, so many abusive arguments in my proximity. Would there ever be relief from any pain i felt? i didn’t know if it was possible. But life continued on.

Life in high school wasn’t any easier for me than life in junior high or elementary. Making friends wasn’t a natural process for me. People found me and added me to their group more than i sought any out. Those of us who were friendly in elementary school stuck together in junior high. It felt like an ease into the right of passage moving from one style of schooling to another. Their friendship was appreciated by me when we were at school but i rarely to never spent time with them outside of those walls. i can still clearly remember what a foggy mindset i had in those years since quitting my barbiturate. Everything seemed to take more energy than i had to exert. Engaging socially felt like a chore. Homework was nearly impossible to do. My mind was inundated with negativity, discomfort, and a kind of mental pain i didn’t know how to describe. The endless chaos of my sister’s life imposing on the rest of my family was always the main focus. Hardly a day went by when Amanda wasn’t causing some grand new crisis or drama which always took my parent’s full attention at home. Their jobs were difficult enough, but then they had to deal with a full time terror with what little personal time they had to spare. Their anger and hatred filled the air of every room in every place we lived in conjunction with those feelings of my sister. I continued to shrink, be the “good kid,” and never talked about anything i was thinking:feeling:doing. There wasn’t space for me. There never was space for me. And when i did try to take some, it was always met with groans and dismissal. i suffered greatly but held on as best i could. There was no other option for me, no relief in sight. i had to learn to endure. My mind and body felt like a prison, negative energy built up into a symphony of distress that never quite reached a crescendo. i had no idea what was happening to me or how to stop it. So my detachment from reality strengthened to a point where being fully disengaged from as much of my life as possible became my norm.

Throughout my life in school, i was stuck in the area of Friendly Weakness. i knew i wasnt ok, but i thought everyone else was. Everyone outside of my immediate family was OK, at least. Intellectually i know that can’t possibly be true. i have vague memories of friends telling me of their family troubles but i didn’t register their problems as anything as serious as my own. Again, i chalk this up to a lack of example and instruction for me. My understandings of other people were stymied by so much. Looking back i can see how much of my interpretations of life were colored by my own inability to manage myself in ways that made me feel good. Knowing what i know of myself now, i can see i had undiagnosed depression, anxiety, gender dysphoria, body dysmorphia, c-ptsd (i am still undiagnosed with this ailment but understanding how my mind attacked:sometimes still attacks itself with past embarrassments and traumas i don’t imagine it would be anything else)...i had no chance of digging myself out of this hole that myself:my environment built for me.

While i performed at school in the most average-to-subpar way possible, the rest of my life was lived online. From age nine i was given unlimited access to the internet, unsupervised. Those unfamiliar with the atmosphere online back in the late 90’s and early 2000’s need to understand: regulation was sparse, and restrictions on content ever more so. There were myriad places to go online that would tell:show you anything you wanted. Real depictions of some of the most heinous crimes humanity could use on itself were on full display next to porn and instructions for making homemade bombs, sometimes all together on the same website. Chat rooms were equal parts fun and dangerous. Connection to strangers behind a veil of anonymity felt more anonymous back then as there were no society-wide examples of social media to be found. People made IRC chats, created forums, filled AOL and Yahoo messengers with their emoticons and favorite blingee designs, and posted lyrics to songs as away messages:forum signatures all without fear that random people miles or countries away would ever know who they are or disturb them. The dregs of the internet on Rotten.com and 4Chan (the latter of which i am ashamed to admit i was a regular user of for several years in my late teens) would use every racial slur you can imagine against one another in their language as a way to bond with one another. My brain and body were full of pain. Of course my mind would find ways to strengthen those bonds, suffering is all my being had ever known.

My relationship to my body changed dramatically at age 14. Where before i had been able to manage my weight without thinking about it, suddenly i was gaining weight in great leaps. My school portraits show the progression down this path as much as my Gateway computer with the cow splatter logo showed the worst parts of me to myself. Again, i agree with RAW in the sense that in the extreme, a body can reflect the strongest connection to the circuit by which it operates. Becoming viscerotonic solidified my place in CI as much as my inability to see beyond answering every immediate need did. My struggles with yet another undiagnosed disorder, binge eating, became another defining factor of my life. I shunned the outdoors, preferring instead the welcoming glow of my television:pc monitor. i would switch back and forth constantly between video games and the internet. Often i would use GameFAQs strategy guides to get through the video games i was playing. I was succumbing to electronic addiction (afterall, my opiate of choice from near-birth was video games) before anyone even considered it a medical issue. People just thought i was a nerd. And i was, that’s true. But i was also engaged in a sort of digital self-flagellation. i was punishing my body in a way my brain could understand since my mind was already too steeped in trauma to know any better way to operate. My cyclical nature was complete. i was who i was online and i had separated myself into a husk of a human being in meatspace (that’s real life to you). i constantly longed for a better way to live, but i didn’t know how to make that happen for myself. So, i never changed.

Having put myself through hell, i barely graduated a secondary high school and found a job. First through my Dad at the pet food factory he worked in, then at Target. Both were as soulless as i felt inside. The only real difference between the two was the amount of raw meat and unidentifiable liquids i was slathered in by day's end. I would cope the way i knew best: video games, movies, television.
