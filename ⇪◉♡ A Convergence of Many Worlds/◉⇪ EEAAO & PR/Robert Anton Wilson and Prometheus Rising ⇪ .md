Born in January 1932, Robert Anton Wilson (RAW) grew up in Brooklyn, New York and was raised as a Catholic. A sickly child, throughout his youth RAW contracted several diseases including polio. He claimed later in life that he found a cure by practicing the "Sister Kenny Method” of treatment for polio. At the time, most people were kept immobile when suffering with this disease. The "Sister Kenny Method" instead focused on using hot packs to warm up muscles and ready them for use, followed by a specific series of exercises meant to strengthen those muscles. For RAW the treatment worked, and he was able to use his entire body in a more meaningful and complete way after a few years of treatment. Most medical professionals during RAW’s youth though "Sister Kenny Method" had no basis in medical truth. It was the first time RAW challenged the status quo, already pushing back against established science as a child. It wouldn’t be the last.

RAW’s upbringing in Catholicism had no appeal to him as a person. He attended primarily Catholic schools until he was able to convince his parents in his late teens to go elsewhere. He wound up attending a Polytechnic high school after lying to them, convincing them he was interested in engineering. It worked. With his newfound freedom RAW visited the school library often, consuming the words of people he never would have found otherwise. He expanded his worldview with the works of poets, philosophers, alternative historians, and fiction writers. The highlight for him during his time in school was discovering James Joyce who remained a favorite throughout his life. Other notable people include Korzybski, Hume, Jung, Keats, Yeats, Faulkner, and Steinbeck to name a few. 

When it came time for college, RAW continued his engineering pursuits, focusing on electrical engineering specifically. Eventually he changed his major to Mathematics and continued to pursue a degree while working full-time. In his personal time during this period he wrote mainly fiction, started smoking cannabis, and had temporary psychedelic experiences which began to shape his worldview further. 

As he got older, RAW experimented with different philosophical identities. He hopped from one label to the next, one ontology (a metaphysical and philosophical framework for understanding the nature of being and reality) to another. Nothing he identified with seemed like the right fit, but that didn't discourage him from searching for answers to life’s big questions. RAW put himself into therapy during his early twenties to reconcile his Catholic upbringing with the personal views of reality he developed in his time away from his family. Eventually he developed a sturdy mental foundation on which to build. Not long after this he was married, and began a new phase in his life.

That phase included building a close friendship with Alan Watts and using psychedelics alongside his regular cannabis smoking habit. Specifically he used peyote, mescaline, and acid, presumably for their known “expansive” mental effects. The books he consumed in his youth shaped his personality, but his life was forever altered once he started this self-described “14 year experiment” on himself with psychedelics. RAW's idea was that he could teach himself to change his brain with a combination of practice and force, based on Dr. John Lilly’s idea of “metaprogramming the human biocomputer.” This entire experiment became the subject of his first book, Cosmic Trigger, Volume 1. One of the key takeaways from a reading of Cosmic Trigger is RAW establishing “Maybe Logic.” The idea is simple to understand, but difficult to implement. 

To summarize: RAW asserts that belief is the death of intelligence, and so intelligent people should put forth the effort to not believe in anything. Belief, being a system of control which has the power to dull both thought and the senses, was something RAW was vehemently against. Considering the parade of religious and otherwise other dogmatic modalities he found himself studying, understanding, adopting, then dismissing, RAW wanted to assert to others the importance that __*there is no one way to understand the nature of reality*__. Each of us has the ability to understand our individual ideas on how being and reality through __discernment__: thinking through our lived experiences and giving thoughtful consideration to what we know and understand. What works for you may not work for anyone else. That’s why RAW claims that the entirety of his writing will work for people “maybe.” In other words, his framework is established on the basis of heuristics. These mental shortcuts use a person’s past history and ability to reason to establish ways of living:reacting to life that are “good enough." They allow someone to make quick decisions and find answers that seem to fit for the moment to maintain their safety. USing heuristics, ideas can be changed and updated at any time as new information arises.

During RAW’s “14 year experiment”, Alan Watts informed him of Dr. Timothy Leary and the Consciousness Revolution. RAW found great inspiration in Leary’s work, as RAW himself took Leary’s Eight Circuits of Consciousness model as the basis for his PhD in Psychology’s dissertation. Though RAW was proud of his work, he wasn’t able to keep his degree in good standing due to the unfortunate closure of his alma mater. In an attempt to make use of his thesis, RAW reworked it into a more palatable form and, just a few years after earning his degree, had this new version successfully published. That published work is known as Prometheus Rising which itself is the basis of the work you're reading now.

In Prometheus Rising (from here on shortened to *PR*), RAW describes the Eight Circuits model as being split into two halves, right down the middle. Circuits I-IV are “primitive,” he explains. These four parts make up the basis of all civilizations at present. Exposing them exposes us to some fundamental truths about ourselves and the worlds we live in. Circuits V-VIII are “futuristic,” expanding the boundaries of human consciousness to give way to greater understanding of the human race and our place in our universe (by RAW's assertion; i assert a multiverse for myself).

The largest section of the work you're reading now is dedicated to the analysis and understanding of PR as critiqued by me. Each Circuit will be explained, scrutinized, then reworked into my own understanding. i have found what works for me and my internal:external interpretations of various systems. i’ll explain how i have reached these conclusions, how they relate to my understanding of reality, and show using neuroscience their locations and functions in a more precise, and hopefully clear, way than RAW does in PR. My hope is that by explaining the work this way it can be read in equal parts through the lens of so-called "Western" science as well as esoteric and metaphysical "woo" or magic.

It’s my belief that RAW assumed a certain level of understanding in those who would read his work. Considering he built it off of a doctorate thesis that makes sense. RAW does imply "Western" scientific ideas throughout PR’s pages. The way he wrote PR could easily be read without this scientific lens, interpreting it as something closer to a theology or cosmology than an ontology. On my first reading, being freshly awake and looking for any spiritual understanding i could find, that was my original interpretation.

My understanding of PR was as something to be adherent to. This is totally antithetical to the book’s intention, and to RAW’s concept of Maybe Logic as a whole. His work is meant to encourage not only questioning everything we ever learn but to mold it to suit our lives or leave it behind completely if it doesn't make sense to us. After several years of practice and life experience i do my best to embody RAW's guidance. By adding the modern "Western" science understandings of the brain to the Eight Circuits, i hope to clarify the way i understand reality now: __*combining science and esoterica gave me a better understanding of reality as a whole than if i chose one over the other*__. i'll consistently argue for this in different ways throughout this work.

∞ i held tightly to my beliefs when i first started working with RAW and *PR*. Eventually my grip started to loosen. Now i think of the lessons i learned from *PR* as ideas i believe and don't believe in all at once. The more i practice my understanding of reality this way, the more i realize that simultaneous denial and acceptance is a quantum function of consciousness. Doing this helps me stay in a superposition of observation and understanding. That is to say: i can never know exactly how reality will react to my understanding of it unless i measure it. The expectations i have for my reality can change the results. If i’m observing some part of my environment, it may or may not change.

∞ For example: i’m sitting at a red light in my car, looking at the light. If i’m observing the light passively, without any expectation for it to change, it may change from red to green in a way that feels fast to me. If i observe the light actively, the mental state from which i observe it can have an effect on the light. 

∞ What if i’m in a rush and want the light to change faster? Does it take the light longer than average to change from red to green because i’m trying to will it to change faster? Or what if i observe the light actively with the expectation it might change at the end of the song i’m listening to? If the light does turn green at that point, has my expectation caused a meaningful connection, a *synchronicity*? Using quantum mechanics as a basis for observations like these, my intuition has created a space for a reaction i can interpret as a measurement. 

∞ From a non-esoteric:metaphysical framework, i also understand that the power of concentration can change how i experience time. If i'm deeply focused on the light, "trying" to make it change by observing it, then the novelty of my action may make time seem to stretch on longer. A focused mind will tend to look for more information to gather if focus is intentional and pointed. If i'm not paying much attention to the stop light then the time may seem to pass more rapidly. Having an expanded focus tends to cause the mind to take in greater amounts of information, but if the current action is common (like sitting at a stop light, something i've done countless times before) then there may not be anything new worth looking for. The mind relaxes and time passes more "rapidly" because it doesn't expect anything novel.

∞ i bounce back and forth between ideas like this almost constantly. Sometimes synchronicties feel like the most real, true thing i could ever experience and other times i remark on how stupid they seem. If nothing else this keeps life interesting. In practice, it might be helping me keep my brain highly *neuroplastic*, or able to change relatively rapidly from a physiological standpoint.

To ease us into the Eight Circuits of Consciousness model, RAW starts with a preface to the Circuits that gives us a strong foundation to build on.
