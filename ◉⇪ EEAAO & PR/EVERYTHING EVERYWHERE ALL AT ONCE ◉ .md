Even if it’s never explicitly stated or shown, i found it easy to read Evelyn as showing classic symptoms of the Gifted Child. Her need to micromanage her life and the lives of her family members is a perfect example of this. When someone has an expectation of greatness put onto them by their parents:guardians, they are expected to never have needs of their own. They must show no weakness. Whether denying their physical needs through putting on a tough facade when they’re hurt or covering up emotional needs by never showing doubt, not crying, or ever asking for help, a Gifted Child is a surrogate parent, an educated genius, a money making machine. Their humanity is expected to be simultaneously superhuman and non-existent. Above all else, they are no longer allowed to be children in any way except physical size. They can either have adult responsibilities placed onto them by force or may recognize the need for someone responsible to take charge of a situation and fill in the gap themselves.

In my early stages of life and Evelyn’s early stages in the film, we were both caught in strict, almost robotic cycles. We adopted:built these cycles as a means of self protection. We wanted to show that we had control of ourselves despite having none at all. Every choice we made was from a place of fear. We never knew safety, so we did what little we could to feel safe. 

For Evelyn, Gong Gong’s disappointment (and the doctor’s delivery message) in his newborn being female is immediate. Evelyn never had a chance to be seen as anything more than a burden unless she followed the strict rules her father set for her. She couldn’t even play outside (or maybe it was playing like:with boys? Probably all of the above) without being corrected. 

Not showing traditional feminine interests, not doing well in school, marrying the first boy she ever talked to because she didn’t know what else to do was enough to have Evelyn pushed out of her own family. Abandoned. Simply because she wanted to be more true to herself.

That feeling of abandonment works both ways, however. Again, though it isn’t shown with Evelyn (since EEAAO skips her teenage years), when a girl is born into some traditional Chinese households the expectation is to take on the feminine roles of the house. Cleaning, cooking, clothes mending, and any other “women’s work” is meant to be shared among the mother and any daughters of the home. In all likelihood Evelyn participated in these expectations either begrudgingly or not at all. 

The only evidence i have for this implication is how “Americanized” both Evelyn and Waymond look when Waymond is proposing marriage on the balcony in China. Chances are both of them had long abandoned traditional Chinese expectations by then. Gong Gong disowned Evelyn for more than just marrying Waymond and leaving China, i’m sure. By that point, Evelyn likely hadn’t been keeping house with her aging mother for some time. Gong Gong’s frustrations with Evelyn are reflected by him feeling abandoned by her, and so he in turn rejects her and Waymond from his family.

How well Evelyn and i took care of ourselves was in direct response to our individual inabilities to work through the stresses of our lives. And since most of our lives were focused on how we interacted with people in our families, we rarely found space to process our feelings. Not that either of us knew how to process them anyway.

Luckily for Evelyn, she had a chance to grow into her life with guidance from both Alpha Waymond and Jobu Tupaki. Me? i wasn’t as lucky. My push into heightened consciousness wasn’t as guided. i was left to fend for my own well being, a choice that in the long term has proven to be the right move. At the time i was worried no one around me would understand what i was going through. i gave myself space from the people i was closest to, to protect not only them but myself from harm.

Conversely, Evelyn’s pain is met with kindness, challenge, understanding, and an interest in inclusion from her established family unit (...kind of). Any guidance i received from people in my life was rare. Much of my understanding came from within:beyond me later in life, led specifically by a chain of media i consumed that made me aware of the work of Robert Anton Wilson.
