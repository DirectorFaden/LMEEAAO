My digital opiate was my savior. By being thrust into the bosom of video games and technology from an age at which i had no hope to self-regulate, i was damned to a disconnected life. What was supposed to become medication instead became a hobby, then an obsession, then an entire life.

My parents, in their wisdom or lack thereof, trusted a child to tend to itself. It’s not ALL their fault, of course. They were raised by people who lived harsh lives and did their best to survive. Those people may never have understood how to manage themselves or their needs. How can someone who lacks the ability to regulate their own needs ever be expected to tend to others? Yet, that’s the expectation. As much as my parents may have failed me, their parents failed them, and our fundamentally broken system of living has failed us all.

And so gaming became my life and the window through which i was able to view the world.

One of the only things i could reasonably rely on for a sense of stability and comfort was the NES and the warm glow of the television. There was always something chaotic happening elsewhere in the house. Other worlds which didn’t include any factors of my own life were only feet away from me at any given time. Who, without proper guidance, would ever be able to resist? i didn’t understand the concept of escapism as a toddler:young child but my brain did.

With an established baseline of medically imposed depression as the means to create a more functional (theoretically, in time) brain, said brain craved a more radical kind of stimulation. Even without a full sensory takeover (think: the way modern VR can provide), my engagement was nonetheless complete and total. With little to no restrictions on my gaming time (as my parent’s belief:faith in the medical advice was total), my use became more frequent. 

The more common skills of reading, arithmetic, and art my mother taught me were being applied to the games i was playing. The games often took those skills and gave me problems to solve with them: logic, resource management, performance under stress, and allowed for multiple attempts before reaching a “failure” state.

To lose a game was not something trivial. A little kid, as i was, doesn’t like knowing they're not good enough to do something. But at least in a video game, if you lose completely you can always start again. Even at that young age i understood that in real life, that wasn’t always the case. Finding a sense of stability through technology practically guaranteed me a foreign mindset to that of my parents. Growing up for them relied more on the people in their lives and their physical environments as opposed to entertainment technology. My childhood was lived almost entirely in the radiant glow of a screen with only myself for company. They were analog technology adept people raising a digital technology adept child.

Even if the means by which i arrived at those conclusions were different thanks to the advent of the digital age, the effects were the same. My intellectual needs could no longer be met by my parents. As a result, i no longer saw them as people to rely on for such things. Instead i looked to the television which, while i knew it was indifferent, was at least stable and consistent. The same could not be said of my parents.

Where my parents could only offer an unstable sense of stability, video games offered stable instability. That is to say: video games are programmed to be finite in their rulesets. There are only so many ways to solve a problem or interact with a game based on its programming. To win is to release anxiety and, should one be compelled, willingly begin the cycle anew to seek that release again. 

However, the feeling of loss compounds. Losing, while disappointing, still allows one to start fresh with a new attempt. Anxiety may remain high and be carried into the next attempt, the tension of trying to win builds, the mental component of the fight presents itself in physical ways through the mentality of the player. Without the tools by which one can reasonably calm:address these problems, they WILL compound. And in an unstable environment, may never resolve and instead be imprinted.

Both fear and hate kept me locked out of a greater understanding of life.
