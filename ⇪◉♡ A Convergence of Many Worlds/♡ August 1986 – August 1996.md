__August 1986 - August 1996__

i was born under a different name in Lakewood, a city in Southern California that isn’t known for much aside from being close to Long Beach. My parents were given a beautiful house to live in with me and, two years later, my sister. The house was given to my family by my grandparents on my Dad’s side who i call Nana and Tata. Even when given such a massive leg up for a successful life, we had our struggles as all families do. Life was far from stable, though my parents did what they could to give the illusion of stability. Emotions ran hot most of the time. Even in moments of calm there was a background of tension permeating the air.

My parents were in their mid-twenties when they married on Flag Day 1986. i was in utero as part of the ceremony. Any semblance of true emotional health between the two of them was shaky. Only a few months earlier my uncle (Dad’s brother) had taken his life in the garage of the house Nana and Tata gave to us. My father was still reeling from the shock of his loss as he walked down the aisle. My mother had never learned to process any of the emotional turmoil of her life. Most notably she wore the death of her father like a chip on her shoulder. He passed when she was young. His absence stuck out to her on their wedding day.

To cope with their realities, both of them were multiple drug users and mutual physical abusers. In the years leading up to the marriage, neither had the awareness or focus to raise children, much less care for themselves. The pressures of their mutual misunderstandings regarding life in general, coupled with the expectations of their parents and families, added even more stress to their relationship. My conception threw an even bigger wrench into the works of their dynamic. As i was conceived out of wedlock, my raised-Catholic father “did the right thing” and married my mother so they could raise me together. They were both doing the best they could.

When i was born, i wasn’t exactly healthy or on-time. Having been born a month premature, there were bound to be a few complications. From an outside perspective, my body seemed fine. My weight was average, my limbs and extremities intact. It wasn’t until ten months later that my seizures began.

The right hemisphere of my brain was drastically overdeveloped. The number of neural connections on that side was nearly double the average which caused my seizures. There was too much interference because of crossed pathways of connection in my brain. Without major medical intervention, i was likely to suffer seizures for the rest of my life. Every indication was confirming that my life would have been cut drastically short without help.

My parents, blessed at the time with good health insurance, were able to take me to a pediatrician at a cutting-edge facility full of forward thinkers and top of the line equipment. My mother was deeply concerned for my health and well being and spared no expense on finding someone who could help me. i was diagnosed with epilepsy and treatment was prescribed right away. For medication i was given a barbiturate, a drug meant for heavy sedation that’s closely related to benzos. Essentially, the plan was to numb my consciousness so completely that it would cause some of those physical connections in my brain to atrophy. The doctor wanted to ease the physical workload of my brain. My mom agreed to this right away. It sounded like relief for me and for her, a chance to give us both some calm in daily life.

The second part of my treatment, as though the first part wasn’t unconventional enough for an infant, was more experimental. The pediatrician prescribed me exposure therapy to stimulus via video games. The idea was that as the physical connections in my brain were culled, i needed to be exposed to engrossing things that challenged me beyond my age development expectations. Add to that a healthy exposure to flashing lights and sudden motion from playing video games and in time my brain would learn to withstand the stresses of daily life. The hope was that my brain would keep and develop meaningful connections based on how it needed to react to the artificial problems it would regularly face. My mom wasn't as sure about this idea. Why would a baby need to play video games to feel better?

The pediatrician found a way to convince my mom that the combined treatments would work better than one or the other by themselves. So under doctor’s orders, my parents bought a Nintendo Entertainment System for me. Heralded at the time as the cutting edge in home video game entertainment, the NES was one of my earliest exposures to electronics. My time with the NES was closely monitored at the beginning.

Starting at just over one year old, i was playing video games for 30 minutes a day with full parental supervision and encouragement. My parents rented and bought games that reflected my young age like Sesame Street learning games, Super Mario Bros., and Duck Hunt. These would be changed out periodically with rentals of Bugs Bunny’s 50th Birthday Blowout, Little Nemo, and Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles. i loved cartoons and Sesame Street, and little me was having fun watching the characters react to buttons i pushed. It was like i was the one making them jump and talk!

What started as thirty minutes of monitored use turned into an hour, then an hour unmonitored, and before anyone knew it, what was once medical advice became a hobby:infatuation:love. i had found my first stimulant drug for my brain: a digital opiate. i quickly became addicted to gaming. It kept me distracted and docile while developing my brain into something useful for me.

Over the years my dose of barbiturate grew along with me along with my taste in games. And as i grew up, my family steadily grew physically and emotionally apart. My parents' multiple drug use turned into frequent physical and mental abuse on both their parts. Their constant arguments over anything and everything affected all four of us, my sister included. My needs for attention when i was young were met by my mother who was staying at home to care for me. Mom made a point to help me develop my academic skills before i was enrolled in kindergarten. By the time i was three i was already reading, writing, and doing math at a first grade level. 

Mom wanted me to be capable, normal, and to fit in at school. Her worry wound up becoming what i carried with me into school. Mom made me exceptional by encouraging me and pushing me beyond my limits. That need for me to be exceptional had good intentions behind it but i soon became what’s now known as a Gifted Child.
