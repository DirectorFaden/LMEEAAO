Though the idea is never explicitly stated or shown, i found it easy to read Evelyn as a Gifted Child. When greeting baby Evelyn into the world and being told her assigned sex, her father Gong-Gong's disappointment is clear on his face. 

What little we're shown of Evelyn growing up includes several instances of her father being over-bearing and controlling of her. If we add in current Evelyn's need to micromanage her life, the lives of her family members, and prove her value and worth to Gong-Gong as an upstanding mother and wife in the present, we can imply how much Evelyn has been molded by the expectations of her father. 

When someone has an expectation of greatness put onto them by their parents:guardians, they may shrink their own needs to find what little love is available to them. Whether denying their physical needs through putting on a tough facade when they’re hurt or covering up emotional needs by never showing doubt, crying, or ever asking for help, a Gifted Child is a surrogate parent for themselves. The child's humanity is expected to be simultaneously superhuman and non-existent, self-managed and invisible. The Gifted or parentified child might also be made to show up for their parents:guardians emotional needs instead of being a parent:guardians focus of care. Children raised this way are no longer allowed to __be__ children except for in physical size. 

Gifted children either have adult responsibilities placed onto them or may recognize the need for someone responsible to take charge of situations. These kids will fill in the gap themselves if they have the ability to. i strongly feel that Evelyn choosing to marry Waymond is her taking responsibility for _his_ life before she made time to discover and build her own. Cultural expectations of her being a good wife and future mother likely played a part in this decision as well. Because she decided to marry Waymond and move to "America", her family disowned her. She wanted a better life and paid the price: Gong-Gong saw her choice as abandonment of the family, so her family abandoned her in return.

That feeling of abandonment works both ways. Though it isn’t shown with Evelyn (since EEAAO skips so much of her early years), when a girl is born into some traditional Chinese households the expectation is to take on the feminine roles of the house as early as possible. Cleaning, cooking, clothes mending, and any other “women’s work” is meant to be shared among the mother and any daughters of the home. In all likelihood Evelyn participated in these expectations either begrudgingly or not at all. The only evidence i have for this implication is how “Americanized” both Evelyn and Waymond look when Waymond is proposing marriage on the balcony in China. Chances are both of them had long abandoned traditional Chinese expectations by then. Gong-Gong might have disowned Evelyn for more than just marrying Waymond and leaving China. By that point, Evelyn likely hadn’t been keeping house with her aging mother for some time.

In my early stages of life and in Evelyn’s early stages in the film, we were both caught in strict, almost robotic cycles of living. We adopted:built these cycles as a means of self protection. We wanted to show that we had control of our selves and our living situations despite not knowing what self-control was. Every choice we made was from a place of fear, lack, doubt, and uncertainty that we were never taught to navigate. We never knew safety, so we did what little we could to feel safe. We learned that the only people we could trust and rely on were our selves.

How well Evelyn and i took care of our selves was in direct response to our individual inabilities to work through the stresses of our lives. There was never a true moments rest in my home because of the emotional and physical turmoil present on most days. Since most of our lives were focused on how we interacted with people in our families, we rarely found space to process our feelings, not that either of us knew how to process them anyway.

In an arguable stroke of luck for Evelyn, she had a chance to grow into her life with guidance from both Alpha Waymond and Jobu Tupaki. i wasn’t as lucky. My push into heightened consciousness wasn’t as guided. i was left to fend for my own well being, a choice that in the long term has proven to be the right move. At the time i was worried no one around me would understand what i was going through. i gave myself space from the people i was closest to so that i could protect both them and my selves from harm.

Conversely, Evelyn’s pain is met with kindness, challenge, understanding, and an interest in inclusion from her established family unit (...kind of). Any guidance i received from people in my life was rare. Though my Mom told me frequently i could talk to her about anything, i didn't feel safe to do that most of the time. Much of my understanding of life came from within:beyond me later on, led specifically by a chain of media i consumed that made me aware of the work of Robert Anton Wilson.
