It was a risk, but the risk of me one day completing suicide because i was holding back my true self was greater.

i invited Mom into my room to talk. i sat by myself on my bed facing towards her, she sat comfortably on my computer chair. i struggled to start the conversation. Where should i even start? What could i say to her that wouldn’t break her heart? i realized there was nothing i could do to lessen the blow. She would have to deal with whatever feelings came up on her own. i started to explain what i had been feeling over the last several years living with my Nana. How my entire existence had been turmoil because of the secret desire i was hiding behind. How my life to that point had felt like a prison sentence where i was destined to endure it for life. How i had never felt good, or right, or happy. 

i’m not exactly sure how long the conversation lasted but everything i wanted to say came tumbling out of me. i wasn’t concerned with being delicate or graceful about how i spoke. There were decades worth of things inside me that needed to be said. Finally i finished my piece. The look on my Mom’s face was that of total devastation. She didn’t know what to say or how to react. She started by trying to console me as i had started weeping heavily in the midst of my coming out and hadn’t realized it. She started up the conversation again with questions. She was curious what it felt like to feel this way. She wanted to know why i waited so long to make this change. She had no idea i felt this way and was remorseful i didn’t trust her enough to tell her sooner, like when i was a teenager. 

When i answered all of these questions and more to the best of my ability she took a moment to reflect. She then told me how much she loved me, cared about me, and wanted the best for me. Those things would never change. What would change was how she treated me, but to my surprise it was that she wanted to change how she treated me for the better. From the start she was willing to learn about trans people, about gender identity and how it was different from sexual identity, about what my life was like. She wanted to support me. She made it clear she would always give me her support and she would always fight for me. “No matter what, you’ll always have me by your side,” she said. “Whatever happens, if your Dad doesn’t want you here i will fight for you. If he wants us both gone, we will leave together. Somewhere, somehow, we’ll find a safe place to live. You deserve to be the person you want to be. I’m having a hard time understanding right now, but your Grandma would tell you that as long as you’re happy and that you’re not hurting yourself or others that you should live the way you want to live. I loved my Mom and her love and support saved me more times than i can count. I’m here now to give the same thing to you.”

We hugged for a long time after this. My eyes were a constant stream, tears streaking down my face almost faster than my tear ducts could handle. i had taken one of the biggest risks of my life and the reward was greater than i had imagined. Acceptance! i never expected that. Familial love remained intact! Something i was told was nearly impossible and shouldn’t be expected. A safe place to live! i had already begun plotting places i could safely park my car overnight that i might live in more relative safety. All of these fears, for the time being, turned out to be totally unnecessary. My heart felt lighter. i felt an inner strength:conviction i had never known. i was prepared to face the world, i knew the path i needed to walk, and now i had more support in place than just one friend who lived 100 miles away from me. It felt like there was nothing i couldn’t do. i was finally on top of my own world. i made Mom promise to keep my secret for now. i wanted to make sure i was better prepared to tell Dad and Nana myself. She agreed, and the months began to roll on.

September gave way to October, October to November, and finally it was December. Mom and i had been keeping my identity a secret while i worked with my telehealth therapist. The therapist and i made sure that i was safe and had some mental tools to call on should the next step of my coming out be less successful. Over the course of this period of time i began telling some of my other close friends who i was becoming. Again to my surprise, most of them were accepting of me and wanted to remain friends (despite quite a few of them being confused). The people closest to me turned out to be the kind of people i needed most in my life. i was extremely lucky to find that my social circles turned out to be every bit as accepting:kind as i had hoped. 

It being December, the end of the year fast approaching, i knew i couldn’t hide my life from the rest of my family any longer. If i allowed myself to cross into the new year without revealing the new me, i feared that it would be something i would never recover from. Over the course of my life, the holidays were hardly anything worth celebrating. Time and again through the years major traumas and tragedies struck between Thanksgiving and Christmas. Deaths in the family on both sides, divorces, separations…there was no familial problem that could resist the draw of my family’s (tenuous) ties between these two holidays. Bearing that in mind i decided i had nothing to lose by adding to this history. It was decided: i would come out to the rest of my immediate family Christmas night. i asked my Dad to set up time with Nana for a video call on Christmas night because “i wanted to talk to her and wish her a Merry Christmas.” Mom was in on the plan, Dad saw nothing odd about the request. Finally the holiday came around.

The way my family celebrates Christmas gave me time to connect to everyone before i went through my big reveal. Our celebrations always began the night before on Christmas Eve. A holiday meal of epic proportions started things off. The honey glazed ham was the center focus, with myriad vegetables, pastas, and potatoes on offer for each of us to have our fill of not to mention both the pecan and pumpkin pies. Following dinner, each of us would give one another one present to open before we went to bed. On Christmas morning the rest of the presents were opened, as well as the overstuffed stockings (hung over the fireplace with care) handed out. I made the best of what i worried would be the last family Christmas i was ever a part of. My stomach flipped and my heart sank many times over those hours, my attention on the holiday happenings thin. Would my Dad and Nana really see me for who i was? Was this coming out really safe to pursue? 

The sun began to set, and i asked my Dad to get my Nana on the phone. Mom and Dad both sat nearby on their couch, me on a nearby loveseat with phone in hand pointed only at me. Once Nana answered, i started to talk. i did wish her a Merry Christmas and asked her how she was doing. With the pleasantries taken care of, i segued as best i could into what i was really trying to do. i told everyone everything i had told my mom before on that late August day months earlier. Dad went completely silent, his face embroiled with varying degrees of disgust, rage, confusion, and fear. Nana began asking questions, seeking to understand what i was saying in a way she could process. She told me how much harder my life would be because of my “choice”. i told her how it was as much a choice as my own skin color was my choice. We talked for maybe ten minutes going back and forth with the focus on my coming hardship and what it meant for me to be doing this “to [my] family.” While she wasn’t welcoming or understanding of 

me at that point, she wasn’t outright rejecting me. It seemed like more than anything she wanted me to understand the consequences of my actions in as many ways as i could.

When the moment felt right, i then told everyone how i wanted to be addressed as a person from that moment on. I told them my name was Kelly, and my pronouns were she/her. Suddenly my Dad broke from his self imposed stupor and came alive with force. He didn’t want to hear any of this, he didn’t want to deal with me being a faggot. He had a hard enough time trying to understand what it meant when i told him i was bisexual. That already felt like more than he could accept. The discussion ended with me holding back tears, afraid he would approach me physically if i let my emotions loose. i thanked everyone for their time and retreated back to my room. once alone i held nothing back and all but cried myself to sleep.

It wasn’t long after this that i found employment in the lowest tier of Amazon as a warehouse staff member.

At first the job was a welcome refresh to my life. Being able to hold stable employment gave me a relief i hadn’t felt in years. The opportunity to use my body for more than sitting provided the feel-good chemicals my body had been craving. Speaking with people who came from all walks of life enabled me to expand my mental horizons somewhat. The only problem was the job was an overnight shift. Relegated to a nearly vampiric life where i was pulling into the parking lot of work after dark and making it home just as dawn broke took a major toll on my well-being. Even with all of these new changes to take on, the overnight aspect completely negated whatever positive changes were slowly coming to me.

The most positive aspect of my time in that position was that it provided a decent wage at the time, and enrollment in a level of health care i had not been afforded at previous jobs. Immediately upon learning i had been hired, i signed up for the health plan and began seeking the right doctors to aid me in my transition. It only took me a month to find someone willing to work with me via a telehealth app (keep in mind this was 2016, well before such a practice had become standard). This psychiatrist, working with my primary care provider, coordinated an antidepressant medication regimen to follow as well as helping me navigate my health plan to ensure i was following the steps they outlined so that i could seek medical transition.

No aspect of seeking medical transition was easy. Even with the ideal psychologist on my side, i had a long journey ahead of me. The health plan demanded that not only do i keep regular weekly sessions with them but, in order to prove that i was committed to actually transitioning medically, i had to present myself, dress, act, and live as the gender i wanted to transition to for an entire year before they would approve any medications or procedures. This was the long game: they were betting i wouldn’t keep the insurance for a year straight, or my sessions with my psychologist, or that i would “grow out of” wanting to transition and they wouldn’t incur the cost. Everything was stacked against me. But i had nothing to lose. My mind was already twisted and frayed from years of torturing myself and my body. My resolve was all i had left. i put it towards what i wanted most: the life i not only wanted:needed but the life i deserved.

i began living as Kelly as soon as i was able. The twisting and fraying of my body and mind didn’t stop, though. Now i was taking on an entirely new set of challenges: what does it mean to be a woman? When you’re 29 years old and Assigned Male At Birth (AMAB), “being a woman” is a performance. The expectations and social realities of femininity revealed themselves in myriad ways. Sometimes sudden, sometimes subtle, there was never a dull moment in the early days of this new life.

Much of what i contended with during this time was the physical reality of womanhood. Accessing female spaces (not just bathrooms but clothing stores, beauty aisles, hygiene aisles, and other designated spaces) was a challenge to overcome in its own right. Being recognized as femme was not an expectation of mine. Being over six feet tall and over 300 pounds left me with no illusion of safety for myself. Worse yet, my stature was immediately read as masculine by most people if not all, and their safety no doubt came in to question for them thanks to propaganda from those that might seek transsexual eradication.

Despite all of this pushback from societal norms and expectations, i still practiced what it meant to be femme. What i can only recognize now in hindsight is this practice being more for the benefit of others more than it was to benefit myself. Every palette i bought, every lipstick, every brush was another tool in the kit of someone who was trying to deceive herself and others in equal measure. The practice i put into doing makeup was minimal despite every session taking multiple hours to complete. i had extra challenges to overcome as well. Without wearing glasses i’m near legally blind, and contact lenses don’t work for me because of my astigmatism as they rotate on my eyeball so constantly that focusing my vision is functionally impossible. Further, i had to either be in front of an open window, have a small fan blowing on my face the whole time, or both due to my skin’s near total rejection of every kind of makeup i would plaster on my face. My pores abhor being covered or clogged and immediately start me sweating so they can clear themselves of any obstruction. This battle for presentable femininity was up so steep a hill that i suspect even Sisyphus would feel bad for me.

In those early days, asserting myself as “woman” felt like an attack i had to practice on others. Most likely this was internalized misogyny: the idea that i, presenting myself as a woman, was a threat to other women simply because i presented myself as one. That also constitutes misandry, the irrational hate of men on the opposite side of the coin of misogyny. What very few people who transition from one gender to another will tell you is that when you transition there is an active and vocal subsect of people who will tell you that you’ll never be the gender you want to be. Their reasoning? Equivalent to that of original sin. “You were born with a penis,” these people might say, “You have no right to talk about femininity or womanhood.” 

This was the kind of messaging i would see regularly on social media. i was especially active on tumblr and twitter at the time, each of them hotbeds of discourse around gender theory. The signals were confusing. On one hand the detractors made transsexual people into a single kind of common enemy. Transsexuality was being “othered,” a dangerous idea that seeks to make those within a specific category subhuman to those that might encounter them. 

As trans people were on the rise so was a resistance against us. Trans holidays are calls for Remembrance, Visibility. Simply asking people to see us as human and not monsters to be 

killed is what we ask for. Every year we see ourselves murdered in online reports in the hundreds which, considering trans people are only 1-2% of the entire population of the United States, is a significant number. Even if only one of us was killed a year it would still be significant in its status as a hate crime. 

This new world i was entering was only new to me. Many before me have endured it. i sought to learn from them and take solace in the fact that, even though my physical reality left me isolated from all other trans people, i wasn’t alone. Our community could be very contentious and wound up devolving into semantic arguments most of the time. But even still there were others like me out there, living their lives as best they could. For a start, that was inspiration enough.

This is where i applied all that i had learned from people like Bailey Jay and Natalie Mars. There was a strength in their resolve that permeated their messages. There was a safety that came from that strength they hardened themselves with. Each of them had built foundations made to withstand even the worst attacks. At the time it was difficult for me to keep in mind the social media phenomenon that we deal with every day: each of us posts our “highlight reel” on our accounts, hiding the ordinary struggles each of us deals with away from that facade. as a result i took on the best aspects of two women who, aside from their choice of profession being considered distasteful to “decent society”, were ordinary human beings who sought safety, connection, and peace like the rest of us.

While i can understand that to the “average” person (again, what does that even mean?) my choice of role model may seem suspect, there’s something you need to understand. When it comes to living in a way that’s honorable and true to the self, REALLY listening to oneself and being in relationship with the deepest part of the self that seeks recognition, there is a tendency to gravitate towards other people like yourself. The marginalized, the outcasts, the others, the freaks, the weirdos, the shunned, the ostracized…all of us together are truly all we have. Only we have the ability to understand each other in a way no one else outside of those marginalizations could ever do. Yes, i make friends with sex workers, transsexuals, people in kink scenes, underground artists, and all others who seek to disrupt the status quo within themselves whether for survival, self-efficacy, or to grow their visibility to a place of acceptance in greater society. Yes, these are my people. All the fucked up faggoty weirdos are my people because i am them. I love them as i love myself because we are the same in many ways. i stand side by side with them in the hope that they would do the same for me. In time, i was proven right in my choice of loyalty. But as a “baby tran” just barely starting to socially transition it was a dangerous choice to make.

All of the complexity of life that i stumbled my way into was something i faced daily, as i said. With that came a new sort of defense mechanism i adopted: self deprecation. That i might “win” the friendship and understanding of the cis (people who identify with their assigned gender at birth) was my greatest driver toward “successfully” transitioning. Knowing nothing else, i would often belittle myself and people like me with jokes that “othered” us all. Shrinking yourself to be seen as acceptable is a very feminine trait. i picked up on that line of thinking quickly and more than anything else i was trying to do with my physical presentation, shrinking won me more acceptance than anything else.

Still, acting this milquetoast wasn’t enough. Who i was as a person was too large and bombastic to keep me in the background for long. Despite the challenges of the job at Amazon i somehow grew a positive reputation among people on my shifts and gained a lot of work friends and acquaintances. The work was still grueling and talking while we worked was not only actively discouraged but punishable. Me being me, i didn’t care. i became part of the people who would regularly dominate a scoreboard of sorts that the management put up for all to see and make a competition of our workloads. As if being drenched in sweat from having to pick up massive 50 pound boxes and moving them 20 feet in less than 10 seconds wasn’t hard enough, we had to COMPETE with each other for recognition. Most people who tried to make this scoreboard burned themselves out and quit. The majority of people didn’t even try and squeaked by doing as little as possible. Me? i only made the ranks because i was doing my best to keep myself distracted from my physical reality, from being seen as “other” by myself and my coworkers. i sold my body that i could sell myself on the idea that, at least for 8 hours a night, it was something i wouldn't have to think about in any way other than mechanically. 

Doing this to myself brought me closer and closer to ending my employment by choice each day i worked. It was getting close to December at this point. i was dreading what a holiday workload would be like. My mind was a muddled mess of emotional turmoil, existential dread, and pure exhaustion. i was giving all i had for a job that didn’t care if i lived or died, as long as i made numbers go up. But suddenly, a glimmer of hope: a brand new kind of position was being created that was still directly connected to the warehouse where i worked but it focused on customer facing needs and problem solving in real time. Suddenly i found an out from my position that didn’t involve quitting. And having proven myself by getting in the top 5 workers each night i was working kept my name in the right mouths. i applied for the position and gave it my all in each of the interviews i had regarding it. i continued to work in my current position while the candidates were being considered. Just as i was beginning to lose hope and debating on quitting in just a few days time it happened: my candidacy was approved and i was hired for this new position. Suddenly the physical turmoil i underwent every day was gone. It was replaced instead with better pay and health insurance, a laptop, and a desk of my own to work from. My life was once again lived in computers. i was all too happy to accept what i thought would surely be the last step i would make within Amazon. The increased stability of my position led to increased stability within me. i had gained a modicum of respect from the people i worked with. Suddenly, my life seemed on an upswing. 2017 was coming to a close and i had hope that the next year would provide me with a better future.
